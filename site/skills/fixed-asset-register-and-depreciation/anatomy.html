How the fixed asset register and depreciation skill is built, and what it does

A 161-line card with seven reference files behind it, split on 25 Oct 2026 from a 1,102-line SKILL.md, then corrected on 27 Oct after a senior-bookkeeper audit found that its proposed entry for a repair found to be capital credited Cash or AP a second time. The skill keeps the fixed asset register, computes the period's depreciation by the user's policy, scans repairs and maintenance for capital items, proposes depreciation, capitalisation and disposal entries, and reconciles the register to the general ledger in both directions. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, what the audit found and how each finding was fixed.

Ten findings, all fixed on 27 Oct, with the standard behind each

An independent senior-bookkeeper audit on 27 Oct 2026 gave the verdict fit with fixes. Each finding was checked against the skill before acting; all ten were fixed in 2d1ec7d, none partly, none rejected.

High · fixed

H1 — The capitalisation correction credited Cash or AP a second time

Step 7's only template was “DR Fixed Asset … CR Cash / AP”, yet it was used for purchases the threshold scan found already expensed. Those had already credited Cash or AP, so the proposal created a second liability and left the repairs expense in place. Catch-up depreciation and closed prior years were not mentioned.

Fixed in 2d1ec7d. Step 7 now has three templates: a new acquisition (DR asset and input tax, CR Cash or AP); an item expensed this year (DR asset, CR the expense account originally charged, plus catch-up depreciation from the in-service date); an item expensed in a closed prior year, not proposed but flagged to the accountant as a prior-period correction.

Behind it · ASC 250 / IAS 8 (correction of prior-period errors); standard reclassification practice references/method.md Step 7 · SKILL.md Stage 4
High · fixed

H2 — The additions and disposals population picked up depreciation and transfers

Debits and credits to “the fixed asset accounts” were taken as additions and disposals. In QuickBooks Online accumulated-depreciation accounts are Fixed Asset-typed, so every monthly depreciation credit would have been listed as a disposal, and transfers from construction in progress as unrecorded additions.

Fixed in 2d1ec7d. Step 0 defines the populations: cost accounts only, accumulated-depreciation accounts excluded, sub-accounts mapped to their parent, additions net of transfers and reversals, disposals excluding them, and each excluded pair shown on the roll-forward's transfers line. overview.md and Gate 3 match.

Behind it · QuickBooks Online chart behaviour; standard fixed asset roll-forward practice references/method.md Step 0 · overview.md · gates.md Gate 3
Medium · fixed

M1 — Without a register no depreciation was computed

The register was Recommended and, without it, “no asset-level depreciation is computed”, although most small businesses have none and the GL history lists every capitalisation.

Fixed in 2d1ec7d. Step 5 offers a draft register derived from the GL history of each cost account, labelled draft register — derived from GL, to be confirmed, computes depreciation from it and reports the difference to accumulated depreciation rather than forcing it.

Behind it · standard practice for rebuilding a missing register references/method.md Step 5 · SKILL.md intake C
Medium · fixed

M2 — The implied-rate check used a denominator that makes mature registers look wrong

It divided recorded depreciation by gross cost per class, which includes land, construction in progress and fully depreciated assets, and needed per-class depreciation that most charts do not post.

Fixed in 2d1ec7d. The denominator is now the cost of depreciable assets in service, less salvage; the test runs per class only where depreciation is posted per class, otherwise in total; the result is an indicator, not an error.

Behind it · standard analytical review practice references/method.md Step 5 · overview.md
Medium · fixed

M3 — No proposal for depreciation missed on assets never in the register

The skill found the capitalisation with no register entry, which it calls the most damaging error, and stopped there.

Fixed in 2d1ec7d. Step 9 computes depreciation from the in-service date for each GL-to-register item, proposes the current-year portion and flags any prior-year portion as a prior-period correction.

Behind it · ASC 250 / IAS 8 references/method.md Step 9
Medium · fixed

M4 — The disposal entry did not balance when output tax was charged

Cash was debited with “proceeds” while output tax was credited separately and the gain or loss used net proceeds, so the entry was out by the tax.

Fixed in 2d1ec7d. Cash or AR is now “proceeds plus output tax”, and the gain-or-loss formula states that proceeds are net of tax.

Behind it · double-entry balance; standard sales-tax treatment on asset sales references/method.md Step 8
Medium · fixed

M5 — Many small businesses book their accountant's tax depreciation

Tax depreciation was “[manual], always” with a hand-off to “the tax return prep skills”, which do not exist, and intake never asked which figure the client actually books.

Fixed in 2d1ec7d. New intake question D2 asks whether depreciation follows the user's own policy or the tax return; if the latter, the accountant's schedule is the register source and no separate charge is computed. The hand-off now reads “your tax preparer”.

Behind it · US tax depreciation practice (Section 179, bonus, MACRS) booked as a year-end adjusting entry SKILL.md intake D2 · references/method.md Step 3
Medium · fixed

M6 — The roll-forward invited FX revaluation of fixed assets

Step 9 had “+/- FX revaluation (foreign-currency assets)” lines while the edge cases say there is no ongoing FX revaluation of historical cost.

Fixed in 2d1ec7d. Both lines are renamed “Translation difference (group presentation currency only — never posted in the entity's books)”.

Behind it · ASC 830 / IAS 21 (non-monetary items stay at historical rate) references/method.md Step 9
