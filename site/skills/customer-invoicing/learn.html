How it thinks

Eight rules the whole skill runs on

Every level below applies one or more of these rules. The examples use one made-up company, Northbrook Trading Ltd. It has a UK VAT registration and keeps its books in pounds. The owner says that UK VAT governs its sales. The UK is one example jurisdiction. The skill works with the rules of any jurisdiction that you give it. Northbrook numbers its invoices in one series, NT-YYYY-NNNN.

Check it yourself

Check your own invoice

The calculator has two parts. Part 1 checks the lines and totals of one invoice. Part 2 checks a list of invoice numbers for gaps and duplicates. The page sends nothing anywhere.

Part 1: the lines and the totals

DescriptionQtyUnit price £VAT rate %Standard price £Last price to this customer £

The page rounds the VAT on each line to the penny. It also calculates the VAT on the subtotal of each rate and rounds it once. The rounding difference is the sum of the line VAT minus the VAT on the subtotals. The page flags a price that differs from the standard price and from the last price to this customer. If you leave one of those two prices blank, the page compares with the other one only. The £250 limit is a UK rule 1. Other jurisdictions differ.

Part 2: the number sequence

The page splits each number into a prefix and a final run of digits. It checks each prefix as one series, from its lowest number to its highest. A voided invoice keeps its number in the sequence. So a gap can be a voided invoice or a deleted one. Ask which before you report it.

Using it

What it asks, needs and gives back

Asks firstDo you want to use Mosofin for this run, or upload the data yourself? Do you want to prepare new invoices or check issued ones? Which jurisdiction governs the supply, and which fields must an invoice show there? For a new invoice: who is the customer, what do you bill, at which quantities and prices, and on which invoice date? Which tax rate applies to each line, and is there an exemption certificate or a reverse charge? What is your numbering scheme?
NeedsTo prepare, the skill needs the customer's details, the lines to bill, your legal name and your tax registration. To check, it needs the issued invoices with their numbers, dates, lines, tax and totals. An item and price list, the tax codes and the chart of accounts make the result better.
Gives backThe skill gives back an invoice draft, marked for review, and the matching journal entry as a proposal. In check mode, it gives back each missing field, each arithmetic error, each duplicate number and each gap.
NeverThe skill never uses Mosofin before you agree. It reads nothing before you answer its questions and say yes to its summary. It never creates an invoice, gives out a number, sends anything or posts an entry. It never assumes the jurisdiction, a tax rate, the invoice date or the numbering scheme. It reads the next number again on every run. It never saves tax IDs, bank details or invoice contents.
