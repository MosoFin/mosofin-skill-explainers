The structure of the 13-week cash flow forecast, and what it does

On main the skill is a 160-line card and seven reference files. The split (fb7f999) kept the text of the original 937-line file. A bookkeeping audit (4523888) then fixed eight findings in the method. Two things set this skill apart. It times each receipt by the customer's measured days past the due date, not by the due date alone. And the lines that breach a floor never come from the books, so the skill always asks for them. Those lines are payroll, debt service, tax dates, the floor and the facility. This page shows how the files connect, the run step by step, and the source behind each step. It also shows the audit findings and where each one stands on main.

The bookkeeping audit: eight findings in the method

An audit by a senior bookkeeper raised eight findings, and the commit fixed all eight. Week 1 now opens at the bank balance, agreed to the books. Outstanding payments, deposits in transit and Undeposited Funds go into Week 1 or 2, once. Recurring amounts come from cash paid, and a duplicate check runs before the chain. Days-to-pay is now one measure: days past the due date, weighted by amount. Overdue invoices go into Week 1 or 2, or past a cutoff from the user, to an at-risk list. Card payments and balance-sheet liabilities due in the window are forecast. A rolled forecast opens at the actual bank balance and leaves out transfers between own accounts. The upload path asks for aging detail, bank balances and card statements.
