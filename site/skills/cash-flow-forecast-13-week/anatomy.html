How the 13-week cash flow forecast is built, and what it does

A 160-line card with seven reference files behind it, split on 25 Oct 2026 from a 937-line file. It projects, week by week, how much money will actually be in the bank: opening cash, the receipts and payments expected to clear, and the closing balance that becomes next week's opening. It names the lowest cash point and its week, tests the floor and the runway, and times collections from each customer's measured payment behaviour rather than due dates. The audit on 27 Oct found that the two numbers that set the low point — opening cash and recurring spend — could both be wrong; all eight findings are fixed on the branch.

Eight findings, all fixed on 27 Oct, with the practice behind each

An independent senior-bookkeeper audit on 27 Oct 2026 gave the verdict "fit with fixes": the structure is professional (direct method, weekly chain, measured days-to-pay, floor and covenant discipline), but starting cash came from the books with no agreement to the bank, and recurring spend was layered on top of open bills. Each finding was checked against the skill before acting; all were fixed in 4523888.

High · fixed

H1 — Opening cash was the book balance, never agreed to the bank

Week 1 opening cash was read from the ledger ("[auto] for the opening balance"). Unreviewed bank-feed items, customer payments sitting in Undeposited Funds and uncleared cheques all make that figure wrong — and it carries through every week of the chain.

Fixed. Opening cash is the bank balance on the start date ([manual] on both paths), agreed to the books through the latest bank reconciliation. Outstanding payments are placed once in their clearing weeks; deposits in transit and Undeposited Funds become Week 1 receipts; unreviewed feed items are listed. Without a bank balance it is labelled "book balance, not agreed to bank". Card and gates ask for bank balances.

Basis: Standard treasury practice: a 13-week forecast starts from the bank balance, with outstanding items listed separately.

High · fixed

H2 — Recurring payments from history were counted on top of open bills

Disbursements came from open A/P, a recurring pattern from vendor-expense history, and last year's one-offs, with no rule to drop an item already in open A/P or a supplied schedule. get_vendor_expenses is also accrual expense, not cash paid.

Fixed. Recurring amounts come from cash paid (ledger on the cash accounts, bill payments). Each recurring item and one-off is dropped where an open bill, a schedule line or a payroll, debt, tax or card line already covers it. Sheet 3 gains a Source column.

Basis: Direct-method cash forecasting practice: each payment appears once, from cash actually paid.

Medium · fixed

M1 — Days-to-pay was measured from one date and applied from another

It was measured invoice date → payment date but applied as days late after the due date, adding the payment terms twice; invoices already past their expected date had no rule, and averages were unweighted.

Fixed. Measured as days past due at payment (payment date − due date), amount-weighted, payments only; expected collection = due date + lag. Past-expected invoices go to Week 1–2 at reduced confidence unless beyond an at-risk cutoff the user is asked for (the auditor's 90 days was not hardcoded). The glossary defines it one way.

Basis: Standard collections forecasting practice; one consistent measure of payment lag.

Medium · fixed

M2 — Balance-sheet liabilities due in the window were not read; card payments were missing

Credit-card balances, sales tax payable, payroll liabilities and loan balances were never read, and the monthly card payment had no line at all — often the second-largest outflow after payroll.

Fixed. Step 0's balance-sheet read now sizes credit cards, sales tax/VAT, payroll liabilities, current loans and accrued interest (amounts [auto], dates [manual]); Step 3 has its own card-payment line; card statements are Required on the upload path when cards are used.

Basis: Standard small-business cash forecasting practice.

Medium · fixed

M3 — The roll did not re-anchor to actual cash, and actuals included own-account transfers

"Roll the forecast forward one week" did not open the new Week 1 at the actual bank balance, so errors compounded; ledger actuals counted a transfer between the entity's accounts as both a receipt and a payment.

Fixed. Each roll opens at the actual bank balance with a variance line against last week's forecast closing; transfers between in-scope cash accounts (search_transfers) are excluded from actuals.

Basis: Rolling 13-week forecast practice as used in lender reporting.
