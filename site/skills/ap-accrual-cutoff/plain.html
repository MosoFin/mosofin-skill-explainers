MosoFin · AP accrual cutoff, in plain words

The goods or the work came this month, but no bill came yet. Is that cost in this month's books?

Yes, if the goods arrived or the work happened on or before the last day of the month. This skill finds those items. It calculates the amount that belongs to the month, and it writes a draft entry and a draft reversal for a person to post.

The 20-second version

You give it a company and a month. It reads your purchase orders, bills and supplier history, from your books or from your files. It asks you where you record deliveries, because most books do not hold that record. It finds goods that arrived without a bill, regular suppliers whose bill did not come, and late bills for work in the month. It prices each one and drafts the entries. It never changes your books.

The idea

The delivery or the work decides the month, and the bill does not

A cost that the business used in a month but did not record or pay yet is an accrued expense. It creates a liability that the business must pay later1. The same textbook matches each cost with the income that it helped to earn1. So a September cost goes into September, even when the bill comes in October.

A liability needs a future payment that results from an earlier business activity or transaction. Money that you owe suppliers for a purchase on credit is accounts payable2. A delivery, or work that a supplier did, is that earlier activity. An order that did not arrive has no such activity yet. That last step is our own reading of the definition.

The answer has two halves. Services that Northbrook used in September become September cost. Goods that are still on the shelf become stock. In general accounting, stock becomes a cost when you sell it. That part is not a rule of the skill.

The mistake it catches

A month that looks cheaper than it was, and an order that is not owed yet

The skill's own file names the first mistake: "The mistake it catches is a month that looks cheaper than it was." It also catches the opposite mistake: a cost for an order that never arrived.

Two common shortcuts

Close on the bills alone, or accrue every open order
Bills alone: September cost$8,050 too low
Bills alone: owed to suppliers$14,650 missing
Every open order: cost or stock$4,200 too high
The first shortcut makes September look cheaper than it was, and October then looks worse. The second shortcut adds 4,200 that Northbrook does not owe yet.

Auditors of US public companies have names for these two ideas in an audit standard3. Completeness means that every transaction that belongs in the period is there. Occurrence means that each recorded transaction happened in the period. A small business does not apply that standard. But the missing cost is a completeness problem, and the accrued order that never arrived is an occurrence problem.

How a run starts

Seven questions, in one message

The skill asks these first, in one short batch. It asks only for what it cannot already see.

  1. Use MosoFin for this run, or upload the data yourself?
    MosoFin is the connection to your books and the other platforms that you link. With it, the skill reads from there. Without it, you upload the files, and the tests are the same. The skill asks only if your account has the MosoFin tools. If not, it says once that the run is manual.
  2. Which period, with the exact dates? Do you keep the books on the accrual basis or the cash basis?
    The answer sets every read. Books on the cash basis hold no accruals, so an opening balance of zero can be correct. The skill never chooses the period or the basis for you.
  3. Where do you record deliveries: a warehouse system, signed delivery notes, a stock module? Do you have any service confirmations or time sheets?
    Goods received is the quantity that arrived × the order price. Most books hold no delivery record. Without proof of delivery, the skill does not accrue an open order. It lists each line that has no proof.
  4. How many days after the month end should it look for late bills?
    Required. The usual answer is "until the books close". The skill proposes that date and waits for your yes. At Northbrook, it is 1 to 10 October.
  5. Which account holds the amounts that you owe but have no bill for?
    Required. Every entry credits this account, and the name differs between companies. On the MosoFin path, the skill finds the account in your chart of accounts and confirms it with you by name.
  6. Can you recover the tax on your purchases, and in which country or state?
    The answer decides whether an entry has a tax line. The skill never assumes the tax rule. It does not reuse a saved rule until you confirm it again.
  7. Is there a materiality threshold? Is it lower at year end?
    The skill can skip items below the limit, and it lists them. If you set none, it accrues everything that it finds and says so.

The basis question matters for tax too. For US tax, an accrual method generally deducts a cost when the events that create the liability occur. You must also be able to calculate the amount with reasonable accuracy, and economic performance must occur4. So the date of delivery matters for tax, and the date of payment does not. That is a US tax rule. Your books and your tax return can differ, so ask your accountant about tax.

What it never does

Six rules that never bend

  • Never reads MosoFin without asking. A connected account is not permission to read it.
  • Never reads anything before you answer the intake questions and say yes to the read-back.
  • Never writes, updates or deletes anything, in any system. Every entry that it makes is a proposal for a person to post.
  • Never treats an open order as delivered without proof. It never uses a supplier spend report in place of the orders, because spend shows only what suppliers already billed.
  • Never chooses the period, the accounting method or the company for you, and never assumes the tax rule. If you set no limit, it accrues everything that it finds and says so.
  • Never counts a cost twice. Each cost is goods received, a late bill or a regular supplier, and only one. Each match of an order to a bill shows the label linked or inferred.
Words you'll hear

Seven terms, in plain English

Accrued expense
A cost that the business used in the period but did not record or pay yet. It creates a liability1.
Accounts payable
Money that you owe suppliers for goods or services that you bought on credit2. Accountants write A/P or AP.
GRNI
Goods received, not invoiced: the goods arrived, and the bill did not.
Purchase order
The order that you place with a supplier. Accountants write PO.
Goods receipt
The record that the goods arrived. Accountants write GRN, for goods receipt note.
Cutoff
The practice of putting each cost in the period where it belongs.
Reversing entry
An entry on the first day of the next period that cancels an accrual. The real bill then posts without a double count.
