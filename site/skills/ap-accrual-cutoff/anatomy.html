How the AP accrual cutoff is built, and what it does

A 161-line card with seven reference files behind it, split on 25 Oct 2026 from a 903-line SKILL.md. The skill finds costs that belong in the month being closed but have no bill yet — goods received not invoiced, a recurring bill that has not arrived, a late invoice for earlier work — prices each one, and drafts the accrual with its reversing entry; it also refuses to accrue an open purchase order nobody received. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, and what the 27 Oct audit found.

Audit → findings

Eleven findings, all fixed on 27 Oct

An independent senior-bookkeeper review of the card and every reference file. Verdict: fit with fixes. The finding that mattered most was the tax default, which overstated expense and accrued liabilities whenever input tax could be recovered.

High · fixed

H1 — "Gross of recoverable tax" overstated the accrual, and the edge-case file said the opposite

The card and method said "the conservative default is the expense gross of recoverable tax, with no input-tax line", while edge-cases.md said to accrue "on the expense amount, not the gross". Accruing tax the entity can recover puts it into expense and accrued liabilities for a month it never belonged to.

Fix. The estimate and the default are now net of recoverable tax, with no input-tax line; the tax is recognised when the tax invoice posts. Where tax is not recoverable it is part of the cost and accrued with it. The read-back says "accrued net of recoverable tax".

Basis: recoverable input tax is never part of cost under IAS 2, IAS 16 and IAS 38, or under standard VAT and GST bookkeeping.

method.md Step 4 · SKILL.md Stage 5, read-back
High · fixed

H2 — Every accrual debited an expense account, including GRNI on stock and capital items

One template, "DR Expense account … CR Accrued Liabilities", for every accrual. A received stock item posts through Inventory Asset when the bill arrives, so the day-one reversal credited expense while the bill debited inventory — and "netting clean" failed.

Fix. The accrual debits the account the PO line will post to — Inventory Asset for stock, the fixed-asset account for capital items, otherwise expense — read from get_purchase_order; the reversal credits the same account. Sheet 2 gains Debit Account and Debit account type.

Basis: goods received and controlled at period end belong in inventory (ASC 330, IAS 2) or property and equipment (ASC 360, IAS 16), not expense.

method.md Step 5, Sheet 2
Medium · fixed

M1 — The GRNI population was cut wrong

"Pull all open POs with receipts in the period" left out goods received earlier and still unbilled; a PO search by the month's dates missed older open POs; and bills netted against GRNI had no date limit, so a bill dated 5 October could reduce September GRNI and also be picked up as a late invoice.

Fix. Every PO open at period end, whatever its date; receipts on or before period end only; only bills dated on or before period end are netted, and a later bill for those goods stays GRNI and is excluded from Step 3.

Basis: standard cut-off practice: GRNI = cumulative received to period end × PO price − cumulative invoiced to period end, per line.

method.md Step 1 · edge-cases.md
Medium · fixed

M2 — Recurring and late-invoice accruals could count the same bill twice

A utility bill for September dated 4 October qualified under Step 2 (no bill in September) and Step 3 (September service, found in the window), and Step 2 tested by bill date, not service period.

Fix. A precedence rule in Step 4: each cost lands in exactly one population — GRNI, then subsequent invoice, then recurring. Step 2 looks for a bill covering this period's service. The card rule and output standard now cover all three populations.

Basis: standard accrual practice: one liability, recorded once.

method.md Steps 2, 4 · SKILL.md rules · output.md
Medium · fixed

M3 — Unposted bills were "not an accrual", wrong for bills dated after period end

"A received but unposted bill is not an accrual, it should simply be posted." A 6 October invoice for September work posts into October when entered, so posting does nothing for September; and Step 3 searched only bills already in the books.

Fix. Only an unposted bill dated on or before period end is "just post it"; later-dated ones go to Step 3, and the user is asked to send invoices received since period end and not yet entered.

Basis: the search for unrecorded liabilities, a standard cut-off procedure built on late-arriving invoices.

Medium · fixed

M4 — The upload path marked bills "Recommended", though two populations need them

The row's own reason showed bills were needed to net GRNI and for the late-invoice review; without them GRNI is gross received value and one of three populations has no data.

Fix. Bills are now Required on the upload checklist, with the consequence stated if they are missing: GRNI cannot be netted and the late-invoice review is not performed, and the output says so.

Basis: the house rule that both paths run the same checks.

SKILL.md upload checklist
Medium · fixed

M5 — No roll-forward or tie-out of the accrued-liability account

The workpaper checked only that last period's reversals posted. Nothing agreed the opening balance, nothing proved the proposed closing balance, and non-AP accruals in the same account were not separated.

Fix. Sheet 1 gains a roll-forward: opening per trial balance, agreed to last period's schedule, − reversals + proposed accruals ± other postings = proposed closing; residual items older than one period listed with owner and action; non-AP accruals shown apart.

Basis: standard close practice: an accrual account is signed off on its roll-forward.

method.md Sheet 1
Medium · fixed

M6 — A multi-currency reversal could use a different rate

"The reversal in the new period uses the same rate — or rebooks at the new-period rate per policy." A reversal at another rate leaves a residue in accrued liabilities that nothing clears.

Fix. The reversal mirrors the accrual exactly, at the same rate; any revaluation is a separate FX task.

Basis: a reversing entry is the exact mirror of the original; rate movement is revaluation under ASC 830 and IAS 21.

  • L2 · The GRNI sheet could not prove its figure. Sheet 2 gains cumulative quantity received to period end, cumulative amount invoiced to period end, and match type (linked or inferred), so each row's GRNI can be read off.
