How the balance sheet reconciliation is built, and what it does

A 161-line card with seven reference files behind it, split on 25 Oct 2026 from a 936-line SKILL.md. The skill ties each balance sheet account to its support — subledgers, schedules, outside documents — and gives every account a two-sided verdict, so an account whose support was never supplied, or is another view of the same ledger, is never called reconciled. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, and what the 27 Oct audit found and how each finding was fixed.

Audit → findings

Seven findings, all fixed on 27 Oct

An independent senior-bookkeeper review of the card and every reference file. Verdict: fit with fixes. The finding that mattered most was that the skill's central rule contradicted its own A/R, A/P and suspense rows.

High · fixed

H1 — A/R, A/P and suspense were "[auto] support" and also "another view of the same ledger"

The card says no account is reconciled to another view of itself, yet Step 1 marked the A/R and A/P support side [auto] from the same books, where the aging ties to the control account by construction. A run either passed a mechanical tie as assurance, overstating how much of the balance sheet is evidenced, or failed every integrated subledger.

Fix. Integrated subledgers get their own verdict: the aging tie is reported as "agrees to subledger (same system)", a mechanical check, and the account is reconciled only with an aging review (name-less journal entries, credit balances, unapplied items, items over 90 days) plus at least one independent test — subsequent receipts or payments [auto], vendor statements or customer confirmations [manual]. Suspense and clearing are tested as "zero, every line explained".

Basis: standard practice for integrated subledgers: the aging-to-GL tie is a necessary mechanical check; assurance comes from reviewing the aging and from independent evidence.

Medium · fixed

M1 — "Write off small residuals to a cleanup account" was a plug by another name

Step 5 said to "write off small immaterial residuals to a cleanup account", and the status list had "Within tolerance". An unexplained residual moved to another balance sheet account is a plug, against the card's own "never plugs a difference".

Fix. A write-off is proposed only below an approved limit the user gives, after the Step 4 checks are documented, to a P&L account named from the chart, with reviewer approval — never to a balance sheet suspense account. The card now asks for the limit and the account; Step 9 saves them.

Basis: common bookkeeping practice for immaterial differences: the cause understood or the search documented, an approved limit, a P&L account, reviewer sign-off.

method.md Steps 5, 8, 9 · SKILL.md Mosofin path, Stages 5–6
Medium · fixed

M2 — The opening balance was never agreed to last period's reconciled balance

The roll-forward started at "Opening balance" and the carry-forward check looked only at items. A back-dated entry or an edit to a closed period would surface as an unexplained difference this month.

Fix. Step 6 gains an opening check: the opening balance must equal the GL balance on the prior reconciliation (or the prior close as read today, said so). A difference means a closed period changed; transactions dated on or before the prior period end but created or modified after it are listed as a possible prior-period adjustment.

Basis: standard roll-forward practice: agree the opening balance to the prior signed reconciliation first.

Medium · fixed

M3 — Customer and vendor balance reports were offered as the support side

Step 0 and the tool table listed get_customer_balance and get_vendor_balance as alternatives to the agings, while Gate 2 and the card say a balance report confirms a total but not the items in it.

Fix. Both are now marked "total only — not a substitute for the aging"; if the aging tool is disabled, the support side is [manual] and an aging export is requested.

Basis: the skill's own Gate 2 rule that a near-substitute is not a substitute; standard tie-out practice works item by item.

Three low findings, all fixed

  • L1 · Common small-business accounts missing from the map. Rows added for Undeposited Funds, Credit Card Liability (to credit-card-reconciliation), shareholder or owner loans and retained earnings, so a run no longer falls back to "ask" for accounts that sit in almost every company file.
  • L2 · Three status vocabularies. One list everywhere: Reconciled / Reconciled — immaterial residual, approved write-off proposed / Investigating / Not reconciled — support not supplied. The card's key verdict now has a place on the account tab.
  • L3 · Sign-off without dates. Section 1 gains "Prepared on / Reviewed on", left blank for the people who sign; the skill fills in neither. Basis: a review without a date is not evidence of timely review.
