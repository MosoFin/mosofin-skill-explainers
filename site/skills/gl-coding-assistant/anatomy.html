How the GL coding assistant is built, and what it does

A 159-line card with seven reference files behind it, split from a 983-line SKILL.md: 155 lines at the split, four more from the audit fixes. It works out which account, expense category and tax code each payment or receipt belongs in, says how sure it is and why, and separates the easy ones from the ones a person should look at. Its strongest evidence is the entity's own coding history, reported as consistency, never as correctness. Audit → findings

Eight findings: seven fixed, one partly fixed, in 7109642

An independent senior-bookkeeper audit on 27 Oct 2026 judged the skill fit with fixes: history with counts as Tier 1 evidence and the “frequency is not correctness” stance are excellent, but the skill had no rule for money coming in, so a deposit could be coded to income when the sale was already booked. Every finding was accepted; one was fixed in part.

High · fixed

H1 — A receipt could be coded to income when the sale was already booked

The skill codes “each payment or receipt”, but nothing told it to check whether an inflow pays an open invoice, deposits Undeposited Funds, or is a processor payout of sales less fees. Tier 1 history (“customer X → 4000 Sales, 20 of 20”) would recommend income with High confidence, double-counting revenue.

Fix. A new Step 4 “Receipts” rule: before any income code, match the inflow to open invoices and to Undeposited Funds or a processor clearing account, and flag “Possible invoice payment / deposit — match, do not code to income”. Processor payouts are gross sales less fees. The upload checklist asks for the AR aging when the batch has deposits.

Basis: standard bookkeeping practice: match receipts before treating anything as new income · references/method.md Steps 0 and 4 · SKILL.md
High · fixed

H2 — The uncoded work list missed everything still in the bank feed

The Mosofin path finds uncoded items by reading Uncategorized and suspense accounts, but in QuickBooks Online and Xero most uncoded lines sit in the bank-feed review queue, unposted. An owner told “38 transactions to code” might have 400 unreviewed bank lines.

Fix. Step 0 and the Uncoded Population sheet header now say the list covers posted items only, that bank-feed items awaiting review are not visible, and that statement CSVs can be uploaded to code them. The read-back example names the limit.

Basis: how QuickBooks Online and Xero hold bank-feed lines outside the ledger until reviewed · references/method.md Steps 0 and 5 · overview.md · edge-cases.md · SKILL.md
Medium · fixed

M1 — “Stop at the first confident match” let Tier 1 skip the capex check

A $3,200 laptop from Amazon matched Tier 1 (Office Supplies, 43 of 45) and stopped with High confidence; the capitalization test sat in Tier 4 and never ran. No rule produced the “Unusual amount” flag the output defined.

Fix. Step 4 now runs on every transaction regardless of tier, and any Step 4 flag caps confidence at Medium. A new Unusual-amount rule flags an amount well outside the vendor's historical range, with the measure stated, or at or above the capitalization threshold. The auditor's fixed “3× median” was not adopted, to keep thresholds out of the skill.

Basis: ASC 360 and the IRS de minimis safe harbor election (expensing versus capitalising) · references/method.md Steps 2 and 4 · SKILL.md Stage 4
Medium · fixed

M2 — The keyword map ignored the direction of the money

“interest” always went to Interest Expense and “dividend” to Owner Distributions, so interest credited by the bank and dividends received were sent to expense or equity.

Fix. Both rows are sign-aware: interest outflow to Interest Expense, inflow to Interest Income; dividend or distribution outflow to Owner Distributions or Dividends Paid, inflow to Dividend or Investment Income.

Basis: standard bookkeeping practice · references/method.md Step 2, Tier 3
Medium · fixed

M3 — The loan principal was “often revealed” by the ledger

Movement in the loan account shows how past payments were booked, not how this one splits; if past payments went wholly to expense there is no movement at all.

Fix. The split comes from the lender statement or amortization schedule, [manual]; the ledger only confirms the post-payment balance. Without the statement the full payment goes to the loan account flagged “split required”, never to expense.

Basis: the lender statement or amortization schedule as the source of the split · references/method.md Step 4, Loan payments · tools-and-glossary.md
Medium · fixed

M4 — Owner and family names were never collected

Personal-use and related-party detection compared payees with the employee list, but owners are often not on payroll and family members and the owner's other businesses never are.

Fix. Stage 1 has a new optional question G for owners, family members on the books and the owner's other businesses, used only for the flag and never stored.

Basis: standard bookkeeping practice for owner-managed books · SKILL.md Stage 1 · references/method.md Step 4
