How it thinks

Fourteen rules the whole skill runs on

Every level below applies one or more of these rules. The examples use one made-up company, Northbrook Trading, a US entity that keeps its books in US dollars. Its September reports come from Expensify. Its policy is version 3, dated 1 January. The policy asks for receipts above $25 and caps meals at $60 a head. It pays mileage at $0.45 a mile from 1 January. These figures are the company's own policy. They are not tax rates.

The report
Mileage

Enter the rate from your own policy, with its date. The skill never supplies one. Leave out miles that have no start, end or purpose. The skill holds those miles.

The page computes total submitted = report lines + miles × rate. Net reimbursable = total submitted − company-paid − disallowed − personal. Amount to pay = net reimbursable − Due from Employee offset. The "Priya Nair, September" preset gives $1,450.00 + 120 × $0.45 = $1,504.00. Then $1,504.00 − $790.00 − $442.00 − $0.00 = $272.00, with no offset. The "Marcus Bell" preset gives $166.59 − $15.99 = $150.60, with no mileage and no offset. A figure from the calculator is a proposal. A manager still approves each line.

Using it

What it asks, needs and gives back

Asks firstDo you want to use MosoFin for this run, or upload the data yourself? Which reports, which employees, which period, and from which tool? What is your expense policy? Where are the receipts? Which mileage rate and per-diem table are in force, and from which date? Which tax jurisdiction and rules apply? For foreign claims, which exchange-rate source do you use? Do you book card charges to a clearing account or to expense? Who approved each report?
NeedsThe skill needs every line of the expense reports. It uses the chart of accounts for coding, and the posted transactions and card activity for the duplicate check. The approved reports not yet paid are for the payable test. You type in the policy, the rates and the exchange-rate source, because no ledger holds them. An employee list makes the result better.
Gives backThe skill gives back six sheets: a summary, the line detail, the exceptions, the posting inputs, the ledger checks and a coverage sheet. It asks before it makes an Excel file.
NeverThe skill never approves, rejects, pays or posts anything. It never supplies a rate, a threshold or an account number from memory. It never reports a receipt as verified. It never saves names, claims or amounts.
