How the expense report processor is built, and what it does

A 158-line card with seven reference files behind it, split from a 1,020-line SKILL.md: 155 lines at the split, three more from the audit fixes. It takes an employee expense report and works out what the employee is actually owed: each line checked against the T&E policy, coded to the real chart, personal and duplicate claims flagged, company-paid items taken out, and the posting inputs prepared for a person to post. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, and the eight findings from the 27 Oct bookkeeping audit.

Audit → findings

Eight findings, all fixed in dc58f7d

An independent senior-bookkeeper audit on 27 Oct 2026 judged the skill fit with fixes: the policy discipline (no remembered rates, “not performed” rather than “passed”, receipts “per the report”) is exactly right, but the reimbursement formula could double-count, and company-card lines were both rejected as duplicates and posted to expense a second time. Every finding was accepted and fixed in one commit.

High · fixed

H1 — The net-reimbursable formula double-counted and double-deducted

Step 4 started from “Sum of all approved lines”, then deducted policy violations and added mileage. Deducting from approved lines removed violations twice, mileage lines were counted twice, and company-paid lines sat ambiguously in the total, so the amount owed could be wrong either way.

Fix. The table is now: total submitted (mileage valued at distance × the supplied rate) − company-paid − disallowed − personal = net reimbursable out-of-pocket − any Due-from-Employee offset, only if the policy allows netting = amount to pay. Net reimbursable must equal the reimbursable column of Sheet 2.

Basis: standard reimbursement practice: the amount paid foots to the line detail · references/method.md Step 4 · output.md
High · fixed

H2 — Company-card lines were rejected as duplicates, then posted to expense again

The match against posted card charges ran on every line, so every legitimate card line on a Brex, Ramp or Expensify report was marked Reject. Step 5 then posted card-paid amounts DR Expense / CR Clearing without asking how the feed was booked; where charges are coded straight to expense, that doubles the expense.

Fix. The against-the-books match runs on out-of-pocket lines only; a company-paid match is recorded as “matched — card charge”. A new intake question I asks how card charges are booked; card lines get DR Expense / CR Clearing only where charges sit in clearing, otherwise a recode proposal at most.

Basis: standard bookkeeping practice for company-card feeds · references/method.md Steps 2, 5 and 6 · SKILL.md I · output.md
Medium · fixed

M1 — The reimbursement-payable “reconciliation” had no test or population

“The proposed net reimbursable, added to the existing balance, should explain that account's movement once paid” is not a test a reviewer can sign, and neither path asked for the approved-but-unpaid list.

Fix. The payable test compares the balance by employee at the report date with approved reports not yet paid, from the T&E tool's list or open employee bills, and lists each difference; without the list it is not performed. The upload checklist asks for it.

Basis: standard balance-sheet reconciliation practice · references/method.md Step 4 · SKILL.md upload checklist · output.md
Medium · fixed

M2 — The duplicate check did not look at earlier reports

The population was the current period and the current report, so a receipt reimbursed on August's report and resubmitted on September's fell outside both.

Fix. The population now includes reimbursements to the claimant for expense dates in the prior 90 days, matched on merchant, amount and expense date.

Basis: standard expense-audit practice: resubmission across reports is the commonest duplicate · references/method.md Steps 0 and 2
Medium · fixed

M3 — No flag for reimbursements that may be taxable pay

Mileage above the tax-authority rate, per-diems above the official table, and unsubstantiated reimbursements are commonly taxable wages, but the skill compared claims to the policy rate only.

Fix. A new edge case: if the policy rate exceeds the rate the user says the tax authority allows, or a line lacks business purpose or a required receipt, flag “excess may be taxable pay — confirm with payroll”. The tax-free rate is asked, never supplied.

Basis: US accountable-plan rules, Treasury Regulation §1.62-2 and IRS Publication 463; UK approved mileage allowance payments · references/edge-cases.md
Medium · fixed

M4 — No evidence of approval before the amount owed was stated

The skill computed the amount to pay but never asked whether the report was approved or by whom; the gates text noted that approval is not in the ledger and then dropped it.

Fix. A new intake question J asks for approval and approver. Sheet 1 says “Approval: per the report / not evidenced”, and self-approval or an approver junior to the claimant is a Review.

Basis: basic T&E control: no payment without independent approval · SKILL.md J · references/method.md Step 6, Sheet 1
Low · all fixed

L1, L2 — two smaller corrections, both fixed

  • L1 — “Policy violation” with no policy supplied. Personal-pattern flags run without a policy but were labelled “Policy violation — possible personal”. They now read “Possible personal — confirm business purpose”; “Policy violation” is used only with a cited policy rule. Basis: the card's rule that unchecked policy is reported as not performed
  • L2 — Merchant history read from a P&L summary. A P&L by department cannot show how one merchant was coded, yet Step 3 cited “coded there 12 of 12 times”. Merchant history now comes from transaction-level reads by vendor; the P&L is used only for the dimensions in use. Basis: traceable support: a cited figure must trace to the read behind it
