How the A/R aging and collections skill is built, and what it does

A 154-line card with seven reference files behind it, split on 25 Oct 2026 from an 845-line file. It answers who owes us money, how late they are and who to chase first: an aging on each invoice's open balance, tied back to the aging summary, a ranked collection plan, the receivables metrics, and reminder drafts that a person reviews and sends. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, what the 27 Oct bookkeeping audit found and how each finding was handled.

Audit → findings

Eight findings: seven fixed and one partly fixed on 27 Oct, with the practice behind each

An independent senior-bookkeeper audit read the card and every reference file; each finding was checked against the skill before acting. Severity is the auditor's.

High · fixed

The aging method was never set to the as-of date, so buckets could be counted to today

The skill named the aging_method argument (Current / Report_Date) but never said which to pass, and the upload row did not say how to export. A month-end aging run weeks later on the Current method ages every invoice that much too old, and Step 3 tied only totals by customer, which match either way, so the error passed the tie-out.

Fix. Summary and detail are read with the as-of date and aging_method = Report_Date; uploads ask for an export with “Aging method: Report date”. Step 3 also compares by bucket and re-pulls on a bucket-only difference, and the recomputed days past due are the authority.

Behind it: standard bookkeeping practice: an aging “as of” a date is aged to that date.

Medium · fixed

The metrics never said whether they ran on gross invoices or the net balance

The population was pulled invoice-only, the summary nets unapplied payments and credit memos, and Step 5 computed total A/R, overdue % and DSO without saying which. A customer with a net credit balance was not handled at all.

Fix. Metrics run on the net open balance that ties to the summary, with gross invoices and unapplied items shown above it. Customers with a net credit balance go to exceptions as “refund or reclassify”, out of the plan and the concentration ranking; the plan ranks on net balance.

Behind it: ASC 210-10-45 and IAS 1 on offsetting: a customer credit balance is a liability, listed separately.

references/method.md · Step 5
Medium · fixed

The tie to the A/R control account was named but never a step

Step 3 said to call the result “a summary-to-detail reconciliation unless a GL control balance was separately tested”, but no step tested it.

Fix. A new Step 3 bullet agrees the net aging to the A/R balance on the balance sheet (or the trial balance on uploads) at 0.01, lists each A/R account, and sends differences to exceptions; the output may say “reconciled to the general ledger” only when it agrees.

Behind it: standard close practice: the aging total is agreed to the A/R control account before it is used.

references/method.md · Step 3
Medium · partly fixed

Asking “accrual or cash basis?” for an aging was a false choice that could stall the run

Intake D asked the question and stopped before reading data if cash was wanted. An A/R aging lists unpaid invoices, so it is accrual by nature whatever basis the owner files on.

Fix. Intake D is now a statement (an aging is always on invoices issued; only DSO and any income figure depend on basis), and the edge case stops only if the user explicitly asks for a cash-basis aging. Partly fixed: the gates.md guardrail was left as written, because it already applies only “if cash basis is requested” and is shared gate wording.

Behind it: standard bookkeeping practice: on a true cash basis there are no receivables to age.

Medium · fixed

Credit sales were always asked of the user, though the books hold them

Intake E required credit sales from the user, and the tools file called Sales by Customer “a credit-sales basis”, although that report includes sales receipts, which are cash sales.

Fix. On the Mosofin path credit sales are invoices less credit memos dated in the period, derivation shown; uploads ask for an invoice-filtered list. The tools file now warns that Sales by Customer includes cash sales.

Behind it: standard DSO practice: the denominator is credit sales, not total income.

Low · fixed

Three low findings, all fixed

  • The wrong reason for flooring Current at zero. Current invoices are not paid early, they are not yet due; the reason now says so.
  • “Current” meant two things, a bucket and an aging method; the glossary now defines the aging method separately.
  • Exceptions had no owner and nothing recorded review. Exceptions now carry Item, Amount, Cause, Proposed action, Owner and Due, and the run context gains a Prepared / Reviewed line.
