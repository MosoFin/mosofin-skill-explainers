How the processor reconciliation is built, and what it does

The skill splits one 862-line file into a 122-line card and seven reference files. It reconciles what a card processor such as Stripe, Square or PayPal charged, kept and paid out against the clearing account and revenue in the books, and tests whether sales were booked net of fees. The 27 Oct 2026 bookkeeping audit found its gross-to-net formula deducting sales tax that these processors pay out; that and nine other findings are fixed. This page shows how the files connect, the run stage by stage, and what both audits found.

Bookkeeping audit, 27 Oct 2026

Ten findings from a senior-bookkeeper review, all ten fixed in b114b88

An independent audit read the card and every reference file as a senior bookkeeper would, and judged the skill fit with fixes. What mattered most: the gross-to-net formula deducted sales tax that ordinary processors pay out, so net settled differed from every payout by the tax. Every finding below is fixed; none was partly fixed or rejected.

High · fixed

Step 2 deducted sales tax from net settled; Step 4 added it back; most processors do neither

“Less: Tax / VAT collected (depending on processor) … Equals: Net settled to entity = $A − B − C − D − E + F”. Stripe, Square, PayPal, Braintree, Adyen and Shopify Payments settle the full charge, tax included, less fees, refunds and disputes, and the merchant remits the tax. The computed net settled fell short of every payout by the tax collected, and booking that variance away would bury a liability.

Fix. Gross is defined once as “charged to customers, tax included”, and net settled = A − B − C − D ± F. Tax is deducted only for sales where the processor or a marketplace remits it, listed separately. Step 4 labels its revenue and tax lines “= gross charged to customers (Step 2's $A)”.

How processors settle (tax-inclusive payouts); output tax is the merchant's liability on the gross sale; marketplace-facilitator rules are the exception. references/method.md · Steps 2 and 4
Medium · fixed

“Disputed transactions still open … not yet revenue” was wrong, and broke the clearing tie

An open dispute does not reverse a sale already recognised, and Stripe and most acquirers withdraw the disputed amount and fee when the dispute is opened, so a GL waiting for resolution missed the processor balance by the open disputes.

Fix. The sale stays revenue. Where the processor has withdrawn the funds, the skill proposes DR Disputes receivable or Chargebacks and DR Chargeback fee, CR Processor clearing, reversed if the dispute is won; a refund liability for expected losses is a policy question it asks.

ASC 606 and IFRS 15: an expected loss is variable consideration (a refund liability), not a reversal of revenue. references/edge-cases.md · open disputes
Medium · fixed

The aggregate entry's clearing line had an undefined sign

“CR Processor Clearing $remaining balance change”. The entry balances only if the clearing line is debited when the amount the processor owes rises, the normal case, so a preparer following the label misstated the clearing balance by twice the change. Refunds were also shown gross, with no tax reversal.

Fix. “DR/(CR) Processor Clearing — increase/(decrease) in the amount owed by the processor”, and refunds net of tax with DR Output Tax Payable on refunds, matching the per-transaction refund entry.

Double-entry bookkeeping: debits equal credits, and an increase in an asset is a debit. references/method.md · Step 8
Medium · fixed

The headline net-revenue finding had no proposed correcting entry

The skill's most valuable finding produced a narrative and an estimated gross-up, but Step 8 listed only routine postings, though the card promises proposed entries.

Fix. A “Correcting a net-revenue posting” reclass: DR Merchant processing fees, CR Revenue for the period's fees from the processor report, and DR Sales returns and allowances, CR Revenue for netted refunds. The output-tax review is flagged separately, with no tax entry proposed without the filed returns.

Gross presentation of revenue and fees: standard practice and ASC 606 principal-versus-agent reasoning for a merchant selling its own goods. references/method.md · Step 8
Medium · fixed

The net-revenue test compared total revenue, not processor-channel revenue

“Revenue ≈ net payouts rather than gross charges”. Most small businesses also take invoice, ACH or cash revenue, so total revenue rarely approximates payouts even when processor sales are booked net: the test passed when it should fail, and a coincidental match could flag a correct ledger.

Fix. The test now compares revenue recorded from the processor (the clearing account's credits, or deposits from the processor payee) with processor net payouts, and says which population was used.

Standard analytical-review practice: compare like populations. references/method.md · Step 7 · overview.md · edge-cases.md
Medium · fixed

“No clearing account” was concluded too fast; in QuickBooks it is often Undeposited Funds

Sales receipts synced from Square, Shopify or PayPal commonly land in Undeposited Funds or Payments to deposit, which is the clearing account. Searching for an account named “clearing” missed it, so the skill would report a false structural error and refuse a possible reconciliation.

Fix. Steps 0 and 4 and the edge case check Undeposited Funds, Payments to deposit and the sync app's account first, and identify the clearing account by where processor sales land, not by its name.

Standard QuickBooks Online bookkeeping practice. references/method.md · Steps 0 and 4 · edge-cases.md
Medium · fixed

The fee-rate check was marked [auto] but its benchmark was never collected

“Fees that don't match processor's stated rate — [auto]”. Intake never asked for the contracted rate and the files say no contract is available, so only the drift of the effective rate was computable.

Fix. Intake gains an optional question F, the contracted fee rate. Step 9 splits the item into effective fee rate by period (trend), [auto], and fees against the contracted rate, [manual], only if a rate was supplied.

The skill's own rule that a test is reported only if it was performed. SKILL.md intake F · references/method.md · Step 9
Low · 3 fixed

Three smaller corrections

  • The bank statement was “Required” while the run continued without it. Now “Strongly recommended — the only proof the payouts arrived”, with the output saying payouts were tied to recorded deposits only.
  • The adjustments sign flipped between steps (“+$F” in Step 2, “− Adjustments” in Step 4), and the roll-forward had no reserve line. Now “± Adjustments” in both, and Step 4 adds amounts transferred to and released from the reserve receivable.
  • “Processor's accounts payable to the entity”. From the entity's side it is a receivable. Now “the amount the processor owes the entity (the clearing balance)”.
