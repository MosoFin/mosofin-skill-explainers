MosoFin · payment reconciliation, in plain words

Does Stripe match your books?

This skill checks that what your payment app — Stripe, Square or PayPal — says you were paid matches what your books say. It also catches the most common bookkeeping mistake with online sales.

The 20-second version

You give it a month. It compares your payment app with your books, line by line, and tells you whether they agree. If they don't, it shows you where and why, and suggests the fix for your bookkeeper. It never changes anything itself.

Where the money goes

A $100 sale never reaches your bank as $100

A customer pays you $100. The payment app keeps its fee, holds the rest for a few days, then sends it to your bank — usually bundled with other sales. Your books have to show all three pieces, even though they happen on different days.

The mistake it catches

Your profit looks right. Your sales are wrong.

Your bank only ever sees the $96.80 payout, so that's often what gets recorded as the sale. The fee is never recorded at all. Profit comes out the same, which is why nobody notices.

How a run starts

It asks five questions before it looks at anything

  1. Use your MosoFin connection, or upload the files yourself?
    Either works. You don't need a subscription to use it.
  2. Which payment app — Stripe, Square or PayPal?
    Each one sends its money differently.
  3. Which month?
    It never assumes a period.
  4. Does your bookkeeper record each sale, or one entry per payout?
    Not sure? It will show you what's in your books, and you decide.
  5. Does your payment app ever hold money back?
    Held money is still yours, so it's shown separately.

Then it reads your answers back — "Checking Stripe against your books for September. Go ahead?" — and waits for your yes.

Words you'll hear

Five terms, in plain English

Payout
The lump sum your payment app sends to your bank, usually covering many sales at once.
Processing fee
What the payment app keeps for handling a card payment. A real business cost.
Chargeback
A customer disputes a charge with their bank, and the money is pulled back from you.
Waiting room
Money the payment app owes you but hasn't sent yet. Accountants call it a clearing account. It should empty out every few days; if it keeps growing, something's wrong.
Reserve
Money the payment app holds back on purpose, often for new or high-risk accounts. It's still yours.
