How it thinks

Eight rules the whole close runs on

Every level below applies one or more of these rules. Northbrook Trading on this page is the skill's own example: a two-member LLC that closes FY2025. It keeps its books in US dollars, on the accrual basis, under US GAAP, in one QuickBooks Online company file. The skill's read-back names FY2026, but that year has not ended yet. So this example moves the year back by one and keeps every other word. Other pages on this site use the name Northbrook with different facts. Do not compare the figures across pages.

Using it

What it asks, needs and gives back

Asks firstUse MosoFin, or upload the data yourself? Which company? What is the fiscal year end? What is the entity type, and for an LLC, how do members share profit? Which framework? Is the trial balance final? Does your system close the year automatically? Is the audit complete? Two-step or four-step? Cash or accrual?
NeedsThe final trial balance at the year end and the chart of accounts with types. For the closed-state test, it needs the trial balance on the first day of the new year and the equity ledger. Last year's statements or return let it agree the opening equity.
Gives backThe closed-state finding, the proposed entries with every account named, both trial balances and the tie-outs. It makes an eight-sheet workpaper only on a yes. The last sheet shows the coverage and names the person who locks the year.
NeverThe skill never reads your data before you answer and say yes to the read-back. It never posts or locks anything. It never proposes an entry before the closed-state test. It never chooses the year end, the entity type or the framework for you.
