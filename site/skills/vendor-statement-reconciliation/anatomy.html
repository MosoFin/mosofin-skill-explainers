How the vendor statement reconciliation is built, and what it does

A 158-line card with six reference files behind it, split on 25 Oct 2026 from a 730-line SKILL.md. The skill compares what a supplier says you owe with your AP ledger, item by item — a genuine outside reconciliation and the main control over the completeness of payables — and explains every difference: an unrecorded invoice, a credit never taken, a payment in transit. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, and what the 27 Oct audit found and how each finding was fixed.

Audit → findings

Ten findings, all fixed on 27 Oct

An independent senior-bookkeeper review of the card and every reference file. Verdict: fit with fixes. The finding that mattered most was that the gap between the statement date and the ledger cutoff was never broken into items.

High · fixed

H1 — The gap between statement date and ledger cutoff was never broken into items

"Treat the gap as a known reconciling window rather than a set of exceptions." With a statement at 25 September and a ledger at 30 September, a missing invoice dated 27 September could be counted as timing, and the Summary's "Explained" column had no items behind it.

Fix. Reconcile at the statement date and list every ledger item in the window individually; first agree the statement's closing balance to its own listed items. The Summary now proves itself: statement balance ± each itemised category = ledger balance at the same date, and Unexplained = Difference − the itemised explained items.

Basis: standard statement reconciliation, done like a bank reconciliation, at the statement date with later activity listed item by item.

High · fixed

H2 — The credit-balance test was the wrong way round for the buyer's books

Step 0 ranked "Vendors with a credit balance in AP" as "almost always wrong". In the business's own AP a credit balance is normal — it is what you owe; an overpaid supplier shows a debit balance. Taken literally, the ranking flagged every vendor owed money and missed the overpaid ones.

Fix. The row now reads "Vendors with a debit balance in your AP (shown as a credit on the supplier's statement)", the emphasis paragraph is corrected, and Step 5 offers reclassifying material debit balances to Other receivables as a proposal the user asks for.

Basis: a debit balance in AP is the standard sign of overpayment or an unapplied credit, normally reclassified to a receivable at period end; ASC 210-10-45 on offsetting.

Medium · fixed

M1 — "Reconciles to the AP subledger and general ledger", with no step that ties to the GL

Every step compared the statement with the vendor subledger. Bills journalled straight to AP, or AP entries with no vendor, never appeared.

Fix. Step 1 first agrees the AP ageing total at the cutoff to the Accounts Payable control account on the trial balance, and reports any gap on the Summary as "Subledger to GL difference", apart from the statement reconciliation.

Basis: agreeing the subledger to the control account is a standard close step.

Medium · fixed

M2 — Balance-forward statements were recognised, with no way to reconcile them

The skill noticed a balance-forward statement and stopped at "until one is converted", with no procedure; many small suppliers send nothing else.

Fix. Step 2 gains the procedure: agree the opening balance to the ledger at the prior statement date, match the period's invoices, credits and payments line by line, and roll forward on both sides — opening + invoices − credits − payments = closing.

Basis: standard roll-forward reconciliation of a balance-forward statement.

Medium · fixed

M3 — The upload checklist could not support the checks the method promised

"AP ageing or vendor ledger" lists open items at one date: no payments, so in-transit and misapplied payments could not be found; nothing after the cutoff; and a ledger for one supplier cannot show invoices posted under a second vendor record, which the card requires checking before "missing invoice".

Fix. The vendor ledger is now required from the prior statement date to today (an ageing alone is not enough), and a vendor list or cross-vendor bill search is required before anything is called missing; output.md lists any classification not run on the upload path.

Basis: the house rule that both paths run the same checks.

Medium · fixed

M4 — "Proposed entries" were named but never specified

Step 5 said "reverse duplicates, reclass mis-postings, record confirmed missing items with support", with no accounts, debits and credits, support or reversal date, and no entry at all for a missing credit.

Fix. Step 5 has a table of entries by case — reversing accrual for a missing invoice with no document, bill, credit, duplicate, reclass, debit-balance reclass — each with its support reference; Sheet 4 gains Date, Account, Dr, Cr, Vendor, Support, Type and Reversal date. All marked PROPOSED — NOT POSTED.

Basis: standard journal-entry documentation: accounts, amounts, support and reversal date on every proposed entry.

Medium · fixed

M5 — "Never reconciled" claimed history the books do not hold

No accounting system records whether a vendor statement was reconciled, and saved runs are empty on a first run, so Step 0 could not know which vendors were "never reconciled".

Fix. Reconciliation history comes from saved runs or the user; on a first run the skill asks for each top-ranked vendor's last reconciliation date and reports "not known" otherwise. It never infers "never reconciled" from the ledger.

Basis: the house grounding rule: every figure traces to a tool result or labelled user evidence.

Three low findings, all fixed

  • L1 · Common causes of a difference missing. A seventh difference type, short-payment or charges — a discount taken, a bank charge, FX, a late fee — detected from payment against invoice, with a write-off limit asked of the user, not assumed.
  • L2 · Cutoff both "Required" and [auto]; Gate 3 re-asking Stage 1. The cutoff is now the user's to state, the profile's year-end only a cue; Gate 3 asks "only those not answered at Stage 1".
  • L3 · Terms without a plain gloss. Open-item, balance-forward and GRNI are glossed once in the card, and "tier" in method.md.
