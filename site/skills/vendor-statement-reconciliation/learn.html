From your books

The page uses expected statement = B − L + T + M − C, and unexplained = S − expected. The page calls it a tie when the unexplained amount is under half a cent. The skill reconciles at the statement date. So L and T hold the ledger items dated between the two dates, plus any payment in transit. The skill itself matches item by item, in its four tiers. This bridge only adds up the totals of what that matching finds. The "Harbor, September" preset puts Levels 2, 3 and 4 on one statement: $18,640.00 + $2,480.00 − $410.00 = $20,710.00.

Using it

What it asks, needs and gives back

Asks firstThe skill asks seven questions in one message. Do you want to use MosoFin, or upload the data yourself? It asks for the statement of each supplier, as a PDF or CSV. It asks which company the statement is for, and the account reference. It asks for your cutoff date and the statement's date. It asks if the statement lists every open item, only overdue items, or a balance brought forward. It asks for a dispute log. Last, it asks which suppliers you reconcile, how often, and who owns the exceptions.
NeedsThe skill needs the supplier's statement, which only you can supply. It needs every invoice, credit and payment for that supplier, from the prior statement date to today. An ageing alone is not sufficient. Before it calls an item missing, it needs your supplier list or a bill search across all suppliers. It needs your cutoff date. Your AP ageing total and trial balance let it agree the supplier ledger to the general ledger. A payment register and a dispute log make the result better.
Gives backThe skill gives back six sheets. They are a summary, the matched items with their tier, the exceptions with owner and age, and the proposed entries. The last two are the vendor ranking and a coverage sheet. The summary goes from the statement balance to the ledger balance, one reconciling item at a time. Below it, the summary shows any difference between the supplier ledger and the general ledger. Every entry carries the label PROPOSED — NOT POSTED. The skill asks before it makes an Excel file.
NeverThe skill never posts an entry and never contacts a supplier. It never books a liability from a statement line. It never forces an uncertain match. It reads nothing before you answer its questions and say yes.
