How the payroll clearing reconciliation is built, and what it does

A 165-line card with seven reference files behind it, split on 25 Oct 2026 from a 1,001-line SKILL.md, then corrected on 27 Oct after a senior-bookkeeper audit found that two of its own automatic tests gave wrong answers on ordinary books: the never-clearing residual read a normal month's carry as a permanent balance, and the year-to-date identity left out the opening balance. The skill reconciles every payroll control account at month end, rolls each forward against its expected balance, ages every balance, tests it against its remittance cadence and finds the stale balances that never clear, including unclaimed pay that may be escheatable. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, what the audit found and how each finding was fixed.

Nine findings, all fixed on 27 Oct, with the standard behind each

An independent senior-bookkeeper audit on 27 Oct 2026 gave the verdict fit with fixes. Each finding was checked against the skill before acting; all nine were fixed in cbfbd99, none partly, none rejected.

High · fixed

H1 — The never-clearing residual was measured at month end

Step 0b took “the minimum balance the account has reached over the last twelve periods” from period-end balances. A monthly-remitted withholding account correctly carries the month's withholdings at every month end, so its normal carry was reported as a permanent balance, the headline finding.

Fixed in cbfbd99. The residual is now the lowest balance reached immediately after each remittance over twelve cycles, less what the cadence says should still be carried; for clearing and suspense accounts, the lowest running balance from GL detail. edge-cases.md and the card match.

Behind it · standard liability reconciliation practice: judge a balance against its remittance cycle references/method.md Step 0b · SKILL.md Stage 3 · references/edge-cases.md
High · fixed

H2 — The YTD identity left out the opening balance

Cumulative accruals less cumulative remittances “should differ by exactly the outstanding liability”. That ignores the balance brought into the year, so the test broke by that amount almost every year, and once corrected it is ledger arithmetic that cannot fail.

Fixed in cbfbd99. Step 6 now includes the opening liability, then ties YTD withholdings per the register to YTD remittances plus closing less opening liability, a two-source test; without the register it says the test proves only ledger arithmetic. output.md and the card match.

Behind it · ledger roll-forward arithmetic (opening + credits − debits = closing) references/method.md Step 6 · SKILL.md Stage 4 · output.md
Medium · fixed

M1 — On the connected path without a payroll platform, the register was never asked for

Intake E fell back to the journal proxy at once, and the Mosofin path's “still ask for” list left the register out, so the connected path gave less assurance than the upload path.

Fixed in cbfbd99. Intake E now asks the user to upload the register when no payroll platform is connected; the proxy is used only if they cannot. The Mosofin-path bullet includes it.

Behind it · standard practice: reconcile to the independent source when it can be had SKILL.md intake E and Mosofin path
Medium · fixed

M2 — “Exact” ageing assumed open-item matching

The skill promised exact ages and item counts, but a liability account is a running balance; remittances are not matched to the accruals they settle.

Fixed in cbfbd99. Step 0b ages by FIFO unless a remittance memo names its period, and the convention is stated on the workpaper; “exact” is replaced by “aged by the stated convention” on the card and in output.md.

Behind it · audit practice: state the ageing convention references/method.md Steps 0b, 7 · SKILL.md · output.md
Medium · fixed

M3 — Write-offs did not separate money owed to an authority from a true residual

The only restriction was not to write off what is owed to a person. An old credit on a withholding account is presumptively tax not yet paid; an old debit is a refund to claim. Neither can be judged from the ledger.

Fixed in cbfbd99. Step 8 requires the authority's or provider's statement of account before any write-off on those accounts: an unexplained credit is a remittance to make, a debit a refund to claim, neither written off. New intake H asks for the statement.

Behind it · ASC 405-20-40-1 (a liability is derecognised only when paid or legally released) references/method.md Step 8 · SKILL.md intake H
Medium · fixed

M4 — Tax remittances were tied per payroll cycle

“For each payroll cycle … tax remittances = sum of withholdings + employer share” shows a difference on every cycle a monthly or quarterly depositor does not remit.

Fixed in cbfbd99. Net pay is still tied per cycle; tax and benefit remittances are tied per deposit period set by the cadence (intake D).

Behind it · tax deposit schedules (monthly, semi-weekly, quarterly depositors) references/method.md Step 5 · output.md
