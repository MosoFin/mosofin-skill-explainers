MosoFin · Month-end close, in plain words

Are the books done? How do I know?

Closing the month means four things. Everything that happened is in the books. Someone checks every balance against something outside the books. The estimates are up to date. And the numbers go to the people who decide, by a date, with a name against each piece.

The 20-second version

You give it the month, the target date, your team and your materiality threshold. It builds the list of close tasks, day by day, and removes the tasks that do not apply to your business. It shows each task's status from what your books show. At the end, it checks that this month starts where last month ended. People run the close. The skill plans it, tracks it and reports. It never changes your books.

A list that fits the business

46 default tasks become 39 for Northbrook Trading

Northbrook Trading is a single US company. It sells professional services, keeps its books in US dollars only and uses the accrual method. It closes October 2026 as a hard close. Day 1 is Monday 2 November and the target close is Day 7, Tuesday 10 November.

The skill starts from a default list. The list has 5 tasks before the close and 46 tasks on Days 1 to 7. Then it reads your chart of accounts and removes the tasks that do not apply. It states the evidence for each removal. So you can see a task that it removed in error and put the task back.

Many of the Day 3 tasks are adjusting entries: accruals, prepaid expenses and deferred revenue. Under the accrual method, you report income in the year you earn it. You deduct an expense in the year you incur it, whatever the payment date2. These entries put income and expenses in the correct period before the numbers are final.

The mistake it catches

A task marked Complete with no entry in the books

On Wednesday 4 November, Day 3, the owner asks for the status. A usual checklist shows what each person says. The skill adds one more column: Evidence. It shows what the books show for each task that makes a journal entry.

The close continues on a false status. The October statements can show too little payroll cost.

A posted entry shows that an entry exists. It does not show that the entry is correct or complete. The task owner still owns the task, and people run the close.

Some tasks wait for information from outside the business. The skill cannot see these tasks and cannot chase them. It shows them in a separate list. At Northbrook, the vendor statement from Crestline Logistics is not yet received.

The final check

Does October start where September ended?

On Day 7, the skill compares two figures for each balance sheet account: the September closing balance and the October opening balance. They must be equal. It checks every account, not only the total. A trial balance that balances proves less. Accounting software makes debits equal credits on every entry.

Someone moved $500 from accounts payable to accrued liabilities. The entry carries the date 30 September. Someone posted it on Tuesday 20 October, after the company reported September. Accountants call this a prior-period reclassification. It may be correct. The owner must know about it before the October statements go to anyone.

A total that also breaks tells more. It means that someone opened a prior period and changed it after the company reported it.

Day 5 compares this month with the budget, the forecast and last month. Management reviews actual results against budgets, forecasts and prior periods. It investigates unexpected results so it can correct them3.

The benchmark covers mostly larger organisations. A small business can close faster or slower. The skill does not set a target. You give it your close date.

After the close, the skill reads the posting dates to find where the close slipped. At Northbrook, the September deferred revenue release posted on Day 6, three days after its Day 3 due date. The August release also posted on Day 6. That is a slip the books measure, so nobody must remember it.

How a run starts

It asks six questions before it reads anything

  1. Use MosoFin for this run, or upload the data yourself?
    MosoFin is the connection to your books and the other platforms you link. With it, the skill reads from there. Without it, you upload the files and the checks are the same. The skill asks this only if your account has a MosoFin connection. If not, it says once that the run is manual.
  2. Which month do you close, and is it a soft close or a hard close?
    Required. A soft close and a hard close need different levels of care.
  3. Your close calendar: the target close date.
    Required. The days in the task list change to fit this date.
  4. Your reporting framework, for example US GAAP.
    Required. It decides which tasks and reports apply.
  5. Your team, the owner of each task, and your close template if you have one.
    Recommended. Your books do not hold the names of the owners. Without them, each owner reads "to assign". The skill prefers your template to its generic list.
  6. Your materiality threshold: the amount below which a difference needs less attention.
    Recommended. It decides which differences need full attention. The skill never assumes a threshold.

Then it reads your answers back and waits for your yes. For Northbrook, the read-back is like this:

"Closing October for Northbrook Trading, a hard close, read from your Mosofin connection. Target close Tuesday 10 November. US GAAP. Owners from your template. Materiality $5,000. One company, one currency, no inventory, so those tasks do not apply. Shall I go ahead?"

It asks whether you want the sheets as an Excel file before it makes one. If you say no, you get the same sheets as tables in the chat. After you see the results, it offers to save the task list, the owners, the dependencies, the materiality basis and the scoping decisions. It never saves statuses or results. It reads them again on each run.

A manager reviews the close and signs it off. The skill only observes and reports. When different people hold authority, custody and accounting, that helps prevent fraud, waste and abuse4.

What it never does
  • Never uses MosoFin without asking. A connected account is not permission to read it.
  • Never reads anything before you answer the six questions and say yes to the read-back.
  • Never locks the books, posts an entry or marks a task complete. It observes evidence and reports it. People run the close.
  • Never treats a posted entry as proof that the entry is correct or complete. The task owner still owns the task.
  • Never assumes a materiality threshold. It asks you for one.
  • Never saves balances, variances, task statuses or close results. It observes every status and every tie-out again on each run.
Words you'll hear

Six terms, in plain English

Close
The work at the end of a month that makes the numbers complete and correct enough to report.
Soft and hard close
A soft close is a lighter close for internal use. A hard close is the full close, with more care.
Accrual
An entry for income you earned or a cost you incurred that nobody has paid yet2.
Materiality threshold
The amount you choose. A difference below it needs less detail. A difference above it gets full attention.
Tie-out
The final checks that show the books are ready to report.
Continuity
This month's opening balance equals last month's closing balance, for each account.
