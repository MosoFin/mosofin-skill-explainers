How the bank reconciliation is built, and what it does

Was one 865-line file; now a 134-line card and seven reference files. It reconciles a bank statement to the cash account in the books, and since the 27 Oct 2026 bookkeeping audit it agrees the opening balance and brings last period's outstanding items forward before it matches anything, so a cheque written in August that clears in September no longer turns into a proposed entry. This page shows how the files connect, the run step by step, what both audits found and how each finding was fixed.

Bookkeeping audit, 27 Oct 2026

Eight findings from a senior-bookkeeper review, all eight fixed in 15893b4

An independent audit read the card and every reference file as a senior bookkeeper would, and judged the skill fit with fixes. What mattered most: the method never brought last period's outstanding items forward or agreed the opening balance. Every finding below is fixed; none was partly fixed or rejected.

High · fixed

Prior-period outstanding items and the opening balance were collected at intake but never used

Intake E asked for “the prior report, or just the date”, but no step used it: Step 0 read the GL “for the period” and Steps 3–4 matched only the period's entries. A cheque written on 28 August and cleared on 3 September landed in “Unmatched bank items”, whose next step is “Propose corrections”, so the run would propose a duplicate expense. Nothing agreed the statement's opening balance either.

Fix. Step 1 “Agree the opening”: the statement opening equals the prior reconciliation's statement ending, opening + lines = ending, and the prior outstanding cheques and deposits in transit are loaded as brought-forward items. Without a prior report, Step 0 reads the GL back to the last reconciled date, as far as the user agrees. Step 3 matches brought-forward items first, and Step 4 has a new category, “Prior-period item cleared this period — no entry”.

Standard bank-reconciliation practice: roll the prior period's outstanding list forward (opening items, less those cleared, plus new ones, equals closing items). references/method.md · Steps 0, 1, 3, 4 · edge-cases.md · SKILL.md Stage 3
Medium · fixed

No test that the books' prior reconciled balance is unchanged

The skill never checked whether the GL cash balance at the last reconciled date still equals the book balance on the prior report. An edit, deletion or back-dated entry in a reconciled period is the commonest reason a bridge suddenly will not tie, and the difference was otherwise chased through the current month.

Fix. Step 1 “Check the closed period is unchanged”: GL cash at the last reconciled date against the prior report's book balance, with any difference logged as an exception before matching.

Standard bookkeeping practice; QuickBooks Online keeps a reconciliation discrepancy report for the same reason. references/method.md · Step 1
Medium · fixed

Deposits in transit and outstanding cheques were not confirmed against the next period

Unmatched items at period end could only be “candidates by date”; the method never asked for the first days of the next statement, which is how a bookkeeper shows a 30 September deposit is really in transit and not missing.

Fix. Step 2 “Confirm against subsequent activity” labels each reconciling item “cleared on [date] per subsequent activity” or “not yet cleared”, and the card's upload checklist gains a recommended row, “Next period's opening activity”, with no fixed day count.

Standard practice and a routine audit procedure: subsequent-receipts and subsequent-disbursements testing. references/method.md · Step 2 · SKILL.md upload checklist
Medium · fixed

Entry templates covered only fees and interest, though Step 4 classifies six other bank-side items

Step 5 had templates for “Record bank fee” and “Record interest income” only. An NSF item reverses a receipt and should not be expensed; a loan debit must be split between principal and interest; a processor reserve is an asset, not a fee.

Fix. New Step 5 templates: NSF or returned item (DR Accounts Receivable, CR Bank, with the bank's fee separate), loan debit split per the lender's schedule and not proposed without lender evidence, processor reserve as a receivable, and chargeback to receivable or chargeback losses per the entity's policy, asked.

Standard bookkeeping practice for returned items, loan amortisation and reserves. references/method.md · Step 5
Medium · fixed

Overclaimed that wrong amounts and wrong dates are detectable from the ledger alone

“duplicates, wrong amounts, wrong dates, wrong accounts, all detectable in the ledger alone”. A wrong amount or date is only wrong relative to the statement or the source document, so a book-side preparation would claim tests it could not run.

Fix. Duplicates and wrong-account postings are [auto] from the ledger; wrong amounts and dates are [gated], proven only against the statement line or source document, and flagged as candidates otherwise.

The skill's own rule against overclaiming; standard practice that an error is proven against evidence. references/method.md · Steps 2 and 4
Medium · fixed

“Every proposed entry names the statement line” could not hold for book errors

Reversing a duplicate GL entry or reclassing a wrong account is evidenced by the GL rows and the source document, not by a statement line, and a book-side preparation has no statement at all. The rule either blocked the skill's strongest no-statement output or forced a misleading citation.

Fix. “Every proposed entry names its evidence: the statement line for bank-originated items, and the GL rows and source document for book errors”, in Step 5, the quality checks, Stage 5 of the card and the description.

Standard practice: every adjusting entry carries its supporting evidence. references/method.md · Step 5, Quality checks · SKILL.md

The escalation ages were stated as rules without saying they are practice

"Deposits in transit older than 10 business days" and "outstanding cheques older than 90 days" are sound bookkeeping convention, but the skill presents them beside funds-availability and unclaimed-property rules that are published. One clause — "these ages are practice; adjust to the entity's bank and state" — now follows the two bullets, and asks for the chosen ages to be saved as a preference in Step 7. The quoted bullets themselves are unchanged.
