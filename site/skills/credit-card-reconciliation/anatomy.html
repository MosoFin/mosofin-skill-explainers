How the credit card reconciliation is built, and what it does

A 144-line card with seven reference files behind it, split on 25 Oct 2026 from a 973-line file, the largest of the reconciliation skills, then corrected on 27 Oct after a senior-bookkeeper audit found the statement-to-GL bridge adding and subtracting its two timing items the wrong way round. What sets it apart is a richer book side: card charges are posted one by one, so duplicates, recurring subscriptions and capital purchases can be tested every month from the books alone, statement or not. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, what both audits found and how each finding was fixed.

Bookkeeping audit, 27 Oct 2026

Ten findings from a senior-bookkeeper review, all ten fixed in 689932a

An independent audit read the card and every reference file as a senior bookkeeper would, and judged the skill fit with fixes. The two high findings produced wrong numbers on every run that used them. Every finding below is fixed; none was partly fixed or rejected.

High · fixed

The bridge added and subtracted the two charge-timing items the wrong way round

Step 2 read “GL balance $X + Charges in GL not yet on statement $Y − Charges on statement not yet in GL $Z + Payments in transit $A”. With a statement closing at $950 and a GL at $1,000 because a $50 charge was booked on 30 September and reached the card on 1 October, the old formula gave $1,050 against $950: a false difference of twice the item, which a preparer might plug.

Fix. The bridge is now X − Y + Z + A ± C ± B: book-only charges subtracted, statement-only charges added, with a new one-sided line C for credits, refunds and issuer interest or fees, and the $950 / $1,000 / $50 worked example under the format.

Standard bookkeeping practice: the liability bridge, GL less book-only charges plus statement-only charges plus payments in transit equals the statement. references/method.md · Step 2
High · fixed

The Option B entry did not balance

“DR Expense accounts — $statement charges / CR Credit Card Liability — $statement closing balance”. The closing balance is opening + charges − credits + interest/fees − payments, so it equals the charges only by coincidence; forcing it to balance either double-counts the opening balance or expenses the payments.

Fix. Both sides are now new charges less credits for the statement period; interest and fees go on their own lines, and a note says payments are recorded when made (DR Liability / CR Bank), not in this entry.

Double-entry bookkeeping: debits equal credits in every entry. references/method.md · Step 7, Option B
Medium · fixed

Statement close and “period end” were mixed in the tie-out

Step 2 tied the statement to the GL “at period end”. For a card that runs from the 16th to the 15th these are different dates, so every charge and payment between them became a bridge item; the skill's own edge case calls this the commonest reason a card reconciliation will not tie.

Fix. A “Tie-out date” paragraph: read the GL liability as of the statement closing date, and separately confirm charges from the close to the period end are recorded, proposing an accrual for any that are not. Step 0, Gate 3, the edge case and the card's upload row all ask for the balance at the statement close and at period end.

Standard practice: tie the liability at the statement date; cut-off for the accounting period is a separate accrual. references/method.md · Steps 0 and 2 · gates.md · edge-cases.md · SKILL.md
Medium · fixed

No step matched statement lines to posted charges, though the bridge depends on it

Step 3 matched receipts to transactions, but nothing matched each statement line to a GL posting, so Y and Z were guesses and a charge entered twice (once from a bank feed, once from a receipt tool) went unseen.

Fix. Step 2 now matches first: exact amount and transaction date within the posting lag, then amount and merchant, then reference, with the method recorded. Unmatched statement lines are Z; unmatched book lines are Y, or duplicates and mis-postings to investigate. The date window is stated per run rather than fixed at the audit's ±3 days.

Standard bookkeeping practice: line-by-line matching before a reconciliation bridge. references/method.md · Step 2
Medium · fixed

Opening balance and statement arithmetic were captured but never checked

Step 1 captured opening, charges, credits, interest and fees, payments and closing, but never proved that they add up, that the opening equals last statement's closing, or that last month's reconciling items cleared.

Fix. Step 1 proves the statement (opening + charges − credits + interest/fees − payments = closing), agrees the opening to the prior closing, and rolls last month's reconciling items forward, escalating any that has not cleared. The card's upload checklist gains a recommended row, “Last month's reconciliation”.

Standard practice: a roll-forward of the prior period's reconciling items. references/method.md · Step 1 · SKILL.md upload checklist
Medium · fixed

Personal charges were flagged but no correcting entry was proposed

The skill identified possible personal use but Step 7 had no template, so the expense stayed in the profit and loss.

Fix. A Step 7 template: DR Owner's draws or Shareholder loan (owner) or Due from employee (staff), CR the expense account originally coded, proposed only after a person confirms the charge is personal; the skill asks which owner account applies. The audit's tax citation was not added to the skill text, which stays jurisdiction-neutral.

IRC §162 (only ordinary and necessary business expenses are deductible), named by the audit; owner's draws and employee receivables are standard small-business practice. references/method.md · Step 7
Low · 4 fixed

Four smaller corrections

  • Option A gross or net. “DR Expense $charges … CR Liability $charges + tax” overstated the liability if charges were tax-inclusive. Now expense at net of tax, liability at the card amount (gross).
  • Edge-case heading contradicted its example. “Card payments from non-business funds” described business funds paying a personal card. Retitled to cover both directions, with a draw or loan to the owner for one and a contribution or loan from the owner for the other.
  • Duplicate test limited to the same day. Card duplicates usually differ by a day or two. Now the same merchant and amount within a few days, with same-day as the high-confidence tier.
  • Gate 3 asked for “Cardholder name” while the card records cards by role. Now “Cardholder (role; a name only if the user offers it)”.
