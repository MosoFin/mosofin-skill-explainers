Ten findings, all fixed in 3814b2d

An independent senior-bookkeeper audit on 27 Oct 2026 judged the skill fit with fixes: the build-and-validate loop is sound, but the multi-currency rule booked an exchange-difference plug and several stock patterns journalled straight into control accounts. Every finding was accepted and fixed in one commit.

High · fixed

H1 — An FX gain or loss plug at initial recognition

The multi-currency edge case said “post in functional currency with an FX gain / loss plug.” At the transaction date no exchange difference exists, so the line was a made-up P&L figure covering a rate or arithmetic error, and it contradicted the card's own ban on a Rounding plug.

Fix. Every line is translated at the spot rate on the transaction date, with the rate and its source stated. If the translated lines do not balance, the rate or an amount is wrong: stop and ask. Realised gains and losses arise only at settlement, unrealised ones only at period-end remeasurement.

Basis: ASC 830-20-30-1 and IAS 21.21 (initial recognition at spot); ASC 830-20-35 and IAS 21.23 (later differences) · references/edge-cases.md
Medium · fixed

M1 — Stock patterns journalled into the control accounts the skill calls red flags

The prepaid, deferred-revenue and intercompany patterns debited or credited Cash, AR and AP by journal, and Pitfall 2 said “the receipt clears AR.” In QuickBooks Online and Xero a journal line to AR or AP does not apply against the open invoice, so the aging and the control account stop agreeing.

Fix. Step 4 now says cash, AR and AP legs go through the subledger (bill, bill payment, receive payment, deposit), with a journal only where none fits and the customer or vendor named on the line. Pitfall 2 applies the receipt to the open invoice as a customer payment.

Basis: standard bookkeeping practice: settlements go through the subledger · references/method.md Step 4, Pitfall 2 · edge-cases.md · overview.md
Medium · fixed

M2 — The loan interest and principal split was said to be readable from the ledger

The text claimed the loan liability's movement “shows the principal portion.” Past movement cannot split this payment, and it may be the very error being fixed.

Fix. The check is now [manual]: the split comes from the lender statement or amortization schedule, and the ledger balance only confirms the post-payment balance agrees. The loan statement is on the upload checklist and asked on the Mosofin path.

Basis: the lender statement or amortization schedule as the source of the interest figure, which must agree to Form 1098 where one is issued · SKILL.md · references/method.md Step 6, Pitfall 5 · overview.md
Medium · fixed

M3 — The open-period check rested on evidence that cannot show it

Entries dated after the posting date were treated as a sign the period was open. Neither a QuickBooks closing date nor a Xero lock date shows in a journal search, and neither intake path asked for one.

Fix. Stage 1 has a new question H: the date the books are closed or locked through, and whether any later period has been reported. It is recorded as user-asserted unless the platform setting is readable; entries after a date are no longer evidence.

Basis: standard close practice: posting into a reported period changes figures already given out · SKILL.md Stage 1 · references/method.md Steps 0 and 6 · overview.md
Medium · fixed

M4 — The accrual double-count check could cause the opposite error

“Always check if a prior accrual covers the same item, and clear it” did not tell a reversed accrual from an open one. Where September's accrual reversed on 1 October, clearing it again leaves a debit in accruals and understates expense.

Fix. The check reads the accrual and its reversal. A reversed accrual means the bill posts to expense as normal; only an accrual still open is cleared on settlement. The reversal policy is asked if the ledger does not show it.

Basis: standard accrual practice: reverse on the first day of the next period · references/method.md Step 6, Pitfall 1
Medium · fixed

M5 — No rule for correcting an error in a closed, reported year

The correcting-entry pattern offered reverse-and-rebook or a net correction without asking whether the original sits in a period already closed and reported.

Fix. A correction to a closed or reported period is dated in the current open period. Immaterial: through the current-period account, referencing the original. Material: against opening retained earnings, flagged for the accountant. Materiality is the user's call; never date into a closed period without their say-so.

Basis: ASC 250-10-45-23 and IAS 8.42 (material prior-period errors through opening retained earnings) · references/method.md Step 4, Correcting prior error
Medium · fixed

M6 — The upload checklist missed inputs the checks depend on

The cash-basis, cross-year and open-invoice checks need the accounting basis, the fiscal year-end and the open items, but the upload path asked only for a trial balance or GL detail, so those checks ran on guesses or not at all.

Fix. The entity row now asks for fiscal year-end and accounting basis, and a new row asks for open invoices or bills (AR or AP aging detail) and, for a loan payment, the loan statement.

Basis: the skill's rule that both paths run the same checks · SKILL.md upload checklist
Medium · fixed

M7 — The QuickBooks and Xero import columns were incomplete

The QuickBooks Online template had no Name column, which an AR or AP line needs. Xero needs one signed Amount column and a tax rate on every line; neither was stated, so an “import-ready” Xero file could load with every credit as a debit.

Fix. QuickBooks Online adds Name, Class and Location, with Name filled on every AR and AP line. Xero uses one signed *Amount column (debits positive, credits negative) and *TaxRate on every line, taken from the user's answer to E and never defaulted.

Basis: the QuickBooks Online and Xero manual-journal import formats · references/method.md Step 7
Low · all fixed

L1, L2 — two smaller corrections, both fixed

  • L1 — Closing-entry detection. The heuristic “P&L accounts at zero after year-end means the system closed it” is wrong for QuickBooks Online and Xero, which never post a closing entry. Now: no closing entry for either; other platforms, ask. Basis: how QuickBooks Online and Xero roll net income into retained earnings
  • L2 — The check count was stated three ways. The model validation line said “9 of 9” over a set of fifteen. Now “validated: 13 of 15 run, 13 passed; materiality not run (no threshold given); loan split n/a”, and the stale “four of the seven” count is gone. Basis: internal consistency
