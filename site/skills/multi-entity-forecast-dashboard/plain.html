MosoFin · group cash forecast, in plain words

We run more than one company file. How much cash does the group make, and how much will it have in six months?

Some owners keep two or three companies, each with its own QuickBooks file. This skill reads the monthly cash flow statement of each file. It adds the files month by month into one group view. Then it projects six months from the last month's figures.

The 20-second version

You choose the company files and the months. It reads each file's cash flow statement, or a file that you supply. It adds the files month by month. It shows the money that your own companies owe each other beside the total, and never hides it. It projects six months on three preset rates. It makes one dashboard file and never changes your books.

What a run finds

Two company files added, one left out, at Northbrook Trading Group

Northbrook Trading Group works in US dollars, on an accrual basis, with a calendar year. Its MosoFin workspace holds three connected QuickBooks files. Northbrook Trading and Northbrook Services LLC keep US dollar books. Northbrook Trading Canada Ltd keeps Canadian dollar books. The run is on Thursday 1 October 2026, and the owner asks for July to September 2026.

For each row, the skill checks one rule. Operating plus investing plus financing equals the net cash increase for the month1. For Trading in August, 38,500 − 12,000 + 0 = 26,500. For Trading in September, 46,200 + 0 − 5,000 = 41,200.

Why is the Canada file not in the group? The skill adds the files month by month, and it has no step that changes one currency into another. If it added the Canada file, it would add Canadian dollars to US dollars. So the example leaves that file out and says so in a note. Your accountant can choose an exchange rate and supply figures in US dollars. Then the skill can use them on the manual path. The skill never chooses a rate for you.

This example makes four choices. It uses the July to September range, and it leaves out the Canada file. It takes the balances from the balance sheet report. It uses the label "Net cash increase" for the 129,100.

The mistake to know about

A total of cash increases is not the money in the bank

The dashboard adds the monthly net cash increases from the first month of the range. It shows the result as "Cash at end of Sep". That figure is the total increase since the range started. It equals the cash balance only when cash was zero at the start of the range1. The starting cash comes from the balance sheet before the period. Starting cash plus the net cash flow of the period gives the ending cash.

Northbrook had cash before July. So the bank balance on 30 September is that cash plus 129,100. The dashboard does not ask for the starting cash.

The same rule applies to the receivables and payables. The skill can add up their monthly changes only when the year started at zero. This range starts in July, so the example takes the balances from the balance sheet report on 30 September. The skill's method says to do that when the range does not start at zero.

The dashboard has a row with the label "Intercompany removed". It takes 9,500 from the receivables and leaves the same 9,500 in the payables.

Group accounts remove all balances and transactions between companies of the same group, on both sides2. US accounting rules agree on this point3. The skill does not prepare group accounts. It shows the total and the part inside the group, so that you can see both. If you need group accounts, ask your accountant.

The skill also checks for test data. It looks for the same figure to the cent in two files and for customer names from the QuickBooks sample company. It also looks for the word "Sandbox" in a company name. The example found none, and its report says that.

The six-month projection

Three rates, from the last month, for six months

The projection starts from the last actual month, September, and runs from October 2026 to March 2027. It starts from the 129,100. The skill takes three monthly rates from the group's figures. The skill names the edges P10 and P90. They are presets of the skill, not percentiles from a statistical model.

September was the best month. So the upside rate, 48,850, is below the base rate, 52,100. The dashboard sets the P90 edge to the larger of the upside rate and the base rate. So P90 equals base, at 441,700. The range is $402,100 to $441,700. Neither path goes below zero, so the dashboard names no first negative month.

The projection adds no more investing, financing or loan payments. Trading paid back 5,000 of a loan in September, but the projection does not repeat that payment. Why not use an average of all months? The skill's method says that an average mixes in unusual months. In this example, the August equipment purchase is in investing, so it is not in any rate.

How a run starts

Four questions, in this order

  1. Use your MosoFin connection to read the books, or supply the data yourself?
    The skill asks this first. If you say no, or have no connection, it uses the manual path with a file from you.
  2. Which workspace?
    If you have more than one, it asks you to choose by name. It never chooses one for you. Northbrook had one, and the owner checked it by name.
  3. One company file, each file separately, or consolidated?
    It asks when you connect more than one QuickBooks file. The dashboard always shows each file and the group, so "consolidated" is the usual answer.
  4. Which months?
    The default is 1 January of this year to the end of this month. For Northbrook that is 1 January to 31 October, with an almost empty October last. The projection starts from the last month, so the owner asked for July to September.

If a read stops partway, the skill tells you what it read. It offers the manual path for the rest. It never fills the gap with figures of its own.

One row for each company and month

  • Columns: entity, month, year, operating, investing and financing cash flow, net income

What it never does

Four rules that never bend

  • No figures of its own. "Every number in the dashboard comes from the tool results or the user's file." No estimates between months, and no typical values.
  • Never hides test data or money owed inside the group. It discloses both every time they appear.
  • Never writes to your books. "Read-only: this skill never writes to QuickBooks or MosoFin."
  • Never changes the look. It adds no chart libraries, no web fonts and no new colours. If you ask for a change that breaks its style rules, it tells you and offers the nearest option.
Words you'll hear

Six terms, in plain English

Company file
One set of books in QuickBooks, for one company.
Cash flow statement
A report of cash in and out, in three parts: operating, investing and financing. The three parts add up to the net cash increase1.
Operating cash flow
The cash that day-to-day trading brings in, after the cash it uses.
Intercompany
Money that one company in your group owes another. Group accounts remove it on both sides2.
Consolidated
All the companies in the group shown as one. In this skill, it means the files added month by month.
P10 and P90
In this skill, the names of the conservative and upside edges of the projection. They are presets, not statistical percentiles.
