How the accruals and deferrals skill is built, and what it does

Audit → findings

Twelve findings, all fixed on 27 Oct, with the standard behind each

An independent senior-bookkeeper audit read the card and every reference file; each finding was checked against the skill before acting. Severity is the auditor's. Three of the findings were material.

High · fixed

The roll-forward test fitted reversing accruals only, so correct cumulative balances were flagged as errors

Step 6 said “The closing balance should equal the current period accrual … accruals shouldn't compound.” A bonus accrued monthly, accrued vacation and interest on a quarterly-pay loan build legitimately until settled; by month 9 a bonus accrual is 9/12 of the pool. A user acting on the rule would reverse a valid liability.

Fix. Reversing accruals close at this period's accrual; cumulative ones (bonus, vacation, interest not yet due, severance) close at the obligation computed at period end, and the entry is the change. A balance above the expected figure is investigated. Sheet 1 tags each category reversing or cumulative.

Behind it: ASC 710 and IAS 19: accrued compensation is measured at the cumulative obligation at the balance-sheet date.

High · fixed

The bonus formula divided by twelve twice and discounted the payout by its probability

The formula read “Annual target bonus pool × the year-to-date portion of the year ÷ 12 … Apply the payout probability”, with a saved example at “80% probability”. Taken literally, month 9 gives pool × 0.75 ÷ 12, and an 80% factor accrues 80% of a bonus management expects to pay in full.

Fix. Cumulative bonus accrual = management's best estimate of the year's payout × months elapsed ÷ 12, or the plan's own earning pattern; the entry is that less what is already accrued, plus employer payroll tax on the same basis, with the estimate's owner named. The probability factor is gone from the formula and the saved example.

Behind it: ASC 450 and ASC 710 (a probable, estimable bonus is accrued at the best estimate of the amount), and IAS 19 (the expected payout).

references/method.md · Steps 3 and 9 · SKILL.md Stage 4
High · fixed

The deferred-revenue entry re-booked the receipt and missed the adjustment small books need

The template opened with “At receipt: DR Cash / AR $gross; CR Deferred Revenue”. The receipt is already in the books, usually straight to income, so posting the draft would double cash; the entry these books need, moving the unearned part out of income, was missing.

Fix. Two cases: a receipt already in income gets a period-end DR Revenue / CR Deferred Revenue for the unearned portion; a receipt already in Deferred Revenue needs no receipt entry and is released as earned. Tax is checked on the existing receipt, not re-booked. Sheet 5 gains contract, service period, earned-to-date and current/non-current columns.

Behind it: ASC 606 and IFRS 15: consideration received before performance is a contract liability.

Medium · fixed

Partial-pay-period payroll was listed but had no method and no input

Payroll accrual appeared as a category in Step 1, but Step 3 gave no computation and intake asked for pay rates and vacation balances, not the payroll register or pay calendar. For most small employers it is the largest recurring accrual.

Fix. Step 3 adds a basis: gross pay for the pay period straddling period end × workdays up to period end ÷ workdays in the pay period, plus employer taxes and benefits. Intake E and the upload checklist ask for the payroll register and pay calendar.

Behind it: standard accrual-close practice: wages for days worked before period end are an incurred liability.

Medium · fixed

Vacation was accrued on days earned, not the unused balance

Step 3 said “earned vacation days × daily pay rate”, which includes days already taken and paid; the edge-case file already said “actual unused balance × pay rate”.

Fix. Unused balance at period end (earned less taken, after any cap or forfeiture) × current pay rate, plus employer taxes, matching the edge case.

Behind it: ASC 710-10-25: compensated absences are accrued for rights earned and not yet used.

Medium · fixed

The output check demanded reversals the reversal policy may rule out

output.md required “Reversals dated the first day of the next period” unconditionally, while intake F and Step 5 allow invoices to settle straight against the accrual, where drafting a reversal would clear the accrual twice.

Fix. Reversals are drafted only where the stated policy is auto-reverse; otherwise the output states that invoices settle against the accrual account. Step 4's reversal sentences are conditional the same way.

Behind it: standard bookkeeping practice: one settlement method per accrual, applied consistently.

Medium · fixed

A foreign-currency reversal could use a different rate from the accrual

The edge case allowed “the same rate, or the current rate, per policy”; reversing at a different rate leaves a residue in the accrual account.

Fix. The reversal mirrors the accrual at the same rate; any revaluation is a separate FX revaluation task.

Behind it: ASC 830 and IAS 21: rate movement is recognised through revaluation, not through the reversal.

Medium · fixed

Opening balances were never agreed, and the upload path left the TB and GL status vague

The roll-forward started from “Opening accrual balance” with no source check, and the upload row was neither Required nor Recommended.

Fix. Each opening balance is agreed to last period's closing schedule, or the output says none exists; differences go to exceptions. The TB and GL row is “Required for the roll-forward and reversal check”; without it Sheets 5–6 are not produced and the output says so.

Behind it: standard roll-forward practice: the opening balance agrees to the prior period's approved closing figure.

Low · fixed

Four low findings, all fixed

  • No payroll-tax line in the entry template, though the edge case required one; added for compensation accruals.
  • True-ups and items not owed. A changed estimate is now a change in estimate in the current period, with restatement only for an error (ASC 250 and IAS 8); an accrual not owed is released against the account it was charged to.
  • Tax accruals had no basis. Property tax is the annual assessment ÷ 12; an income tax provision is taken from the user or the tax preparer, and computing it (ASC 740 and IAS 12) is out of scope.
  • [gated] meant two things. Judgment steps now read “[manual] (judgment)”, and commission and interest show their [auto] and [manual] parts.
