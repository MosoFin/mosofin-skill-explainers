How the AP aging and payment run is built, and what it does

A 156-line card with seven reference files behind it, split on 25 Oct 2026 from a 911-line file. It answers two questions: who do we owe and how late are we, as an aging tied to the A/P control balance; and, given the cash the user confirms for this week, who to pay first, as a proposed payment run that stops when the cash runs out. It posts and pays nothing. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, what the 27 Oct bookkeeping audit found and how each finding was fixed.

Audit → findings

Ten findings, all fixed on 27 Oct, with the practice behind each

An independent senior-bookkeeper audit read the card and every reference file; each finding was checked against the skill before acting. Severity is the auditor's.

High · fixed

Vendor credits and unapplied payments were thrown away, so the aging could not tie and vendors were paid gross

Step 1 said “Confirm open amount > 0 (skip fully paid or fully credited bills)”. Unapplied vendor credits and payments carry negative open amounts and sit inside the A/P control balance, so dropping them made the aging exceed the control balance by the credits, and Step 5 then paid each vendor's bills gross, which the skill's own edge cases forbid.

Fix. Every non-zero open item is kept; unapplied credits and payments stay in the vendor's row as negatives. Step 5 nets each vendor's open credits against its selected bills and flags a vendor whose credits exceed its bills as a debit balance. The card's Stage 3 wording matches.

Behind it: standard bookkeeping practice: the A/P aging agrees to the A/P control account, with open credits shown as negative lines in the vendor's row.

Medium · fixed

Discounts taken were not recorded, so the 2% stayed open and came back next run

The run proposed paying 98% of a bill with no instruction for recording the 2%; a payment entered for the net amount alone leaves the bill open for the discount. Sheet 3 also left unclear which column counted against the cash constraint.

Fix. Where a discount is taken the line shows bill open amount, discount and cash paid, and the discount is recorded with the payment to the purchase-discount account named from the chart. Sheet 3's columns are renamed and the cash constraint is on cash paid.

Behind it: standard bookkeeping practice: a purchase discount is recorded with the payment, so the bill closes in full.

Medium · fixed

Payments in flight risked a double deduction, and payments made outside the books were never asked for

The edge case reduced a bill by in-process payments the books already showed, which are already in the open amount. The real risk, payments scheduled in a bill-pay platform or bank portal but not yet recorded, was never asked about.

Fix. Recorded payments are not deducted again. Intake question E now asks for payments already scheduled or sent that are not in the books, and those are deducted, labelled user-supplied.

Behind it: standard accounts-payable control against duplicate payment.

references/edge-cases.md · SKILL.md intake E
Medium · fixed

The duplicate test missed the most common duplicate

The test required “Same vendor + same amount + same date appearing twice”. Duplicates are usually the same invoice entered on different dates, once from the emailed PDF and once from a statement.

Fix. Duplicates are now same vendor and same bill number, ignoring punctuation and leading zeros, or same vendor and same amount on close dates outside the normal billing cycle. No fixed day window is hard-coded.

Behind it: standard accounts-payable duplicate testing (matching on vendor and invoice number, then vendor and amount).

references/method.md · Step 1
Medium · fixed

The bank-detail fraud check had nothing to trigger it

The card promised to catch “a payment sent to bank details a fraudster just ‘updated’”, but the skill never sees bank details and no question asked whether any had changed.

Fix. Intake E asks whether any vendor in the run has asked to change bank details since their last payment. Each one named is flagged on Sheet 3 to be held until confirmed on a number from the user's own records; if the question goes unanswered, Sheet 3 says “bank-detail changes not screened”.

Behind it: standard payment-fraud control: verify a change of bank details by calling back on a number already on file, never one supplied in the request.

Medium · fixed

On the upload path the A/P control balance was optional, though the tie-out is the card's headline

The checklist marked the control balance “Recommended”, and nothing said the output must then state that the aging was not tied.

Fix. The row is now “Required for the tie-out”; without it Sheet 1 is headed “not agreed to the A/P control balance” and the read-back says so.

Behind it: standard bookkeeping practice: a subledger is agreed to its general-ledger control account before it is relied on.

Four low findings, all fixed

  • [gated] meant two things. Steps 4–6 used it for business judgment as well as for permission-gated tools; judgment steps now read “[manual] (judgment)”.
  • “Days outstanding” meant days past due, and a bill due today had no bucket; renamed to the glossary term, and zero counts as not yet due.
  • No Held tab and no sign-off. The edge cases referred to a “Held” tab the output lacked; Sheet 3b Held and Disputed was added, and Sheet 3 gained Prepared by / Approved by lines.
  • The discount return used the approximation. Now the exact rate d/(1−d) × 365/(N−D), 37.2% for 2/10 Net 30, with 36.5% labelled approximate; the ranking does not change.
