MosoFin · AP aging and payment runs, in plain words

Who do we pay this week, with the cash we actually have?

Every week a business has more bills than cash. This skill sorts every unpaid bill by how late it is. Then it proposes who to pay first with the cash you choose to spend this week.

The 20-second version

You give it a date and the amount of cash you choose to spend. It reads your open bills, terms and vendors, from your books or from your files. It sorts each bill into a band of lateness. It finds discounts that end soon and late fees that grow. It ranks the bills and stops when the next bill does not fit the cash. It shows what stays unpaid. The run is a proposal. A person approves it and pays it in the bank or accounting system.

What a run finds

Nine bills at Northbrook Trading on Friday 6 November

Northbrook Trading pays its bills once a week. The as-of date is Friday 6 November 2026. Northbrook has nine open bills, all in US dollars. The skill calculates the days past due from the as-of date for each bill. Then it puts each bill in a band of lateness. Accountants call these bands aging buckets.

Who gets paid first

$62,850 of bills and $40,000 to spend

Northbrook has more money in the bank. The owner keeps $10,000 in reserve and chooses to spend $40,000 this week. That $40,000 is the cash available for the run. It is what the owner chooses to spend, not the bank balance. The skill asks for this figure every run.

The skill ranks the bills in its default order. It adds each bill to the run until the next bill does not fit the cash.

The skill shows the unpaid bills on the face of the result, not in a note. Those are the bills you must make a decision about.

Why does a small discount rank above a bill that is due? Harbor gives 2% for payment within 10 days, instead of 30. The skill calculates the return a year as 2% × 365 / (30 − 10) = 36.5%. A finance textbook calculates the cost of a missed 2/10, net 30 discount as 36.73% a year, with a slightly fuller formula1. Both give about 36% a year.

Crestline's terms add 1.5% a month to a late bill: 12,500 × 1.5% = $187.50 a month. So Crestline ranks above the larger Pinnacle bill, which costs nothing to wait on. The skill takes a late fee from the terms in your books. If the fee is only in a contract, it asks you.

The mistake it catches

The email that asks you to pay a new bank account

Lakeside Freight is late and sent a final notice. On Tuesday 3 November, an email came "from Lakeside" with new bank details. A request to change bank details is a common way for businesses to lose money. The skill flags the change and does not use the new details.

The FBI gives the same advice. Confirm a request to change payment or account details through a second channel, such as a phone number you already have. Do not use a number or link in the request2.

Most changes of bank details are real. The skill does not know which kind it found. It flags the change, and you decide after you confirm it through a second channel.

How a run starts

It asks six questions before it reads anything

  1. Use MosoFin for this run, or upload the data yourself?
    MosoFin is the connection to your books and the other platforms you link. With it, the skill reads from there. Without it, you upload the files and the skill does the same work. The skill asks this only if your account has a MosoFin connection. If not, it says once that the run is manual.
  2. The as-of date for the aging.
    Required. The aging changes from day to day. The skill never picks a date for you.
  3. How much cash is available for this payment run?
    Required for the payment run. The run stops when the cash ends. Give the amount after payments you already promised, your float and any reserve. Without it, the skill builds the aging only. It never reads a bank balance and calls it available.
  4. Which vendors are critical, standard or discretionary?
    Optional. Your books do not know which supplier stops service first. Without the tiers, the skill ranks on dates, discounts and late fees only, and says so.
  5. How you pay each vendor, and any terms or holds you keep outside the books.
    Recommended. The books hold the names of the payment methods. They often do not show which vendor gets which method. Without your answer, that column reads "to confirm".
  6. If your bills are in more than one currency: the exchange rate and its source.
    Required if several currencies appear. No accounting connection supplies a live rate for a bank transfer. The skill never assumes a rate.

Then it reads your answers back and waits for your yes. For Northbrook, the read-back is like this:

"Aging as at Friday 6 November for Northbrook Trading, read from your Mosofin connection. Payment run for $40,000 of available cash, after the $10,000 you keep back. Power Co and PayrollCo are critical. Default buckets. All bills in USD. Shall I go ahead?"

It asks whether you want the sheets as an Excel file before it makes one. If you say no, you get the same sheets as tables in the chat. After you see the results, it offers to save your vendor tiers, payment methods, holds and ranking order for the next run. It never saves bank details or the cash available.

What it never does
  • Never reads your books without asking. A connected account is not permission.
  • Never reads anything before you answer the six questions and say yes to the read-back.
  • Never sends, releases or schedules a payment, changes bank details or puts a vendor on hold. The run is a proposal, headed as one. A person approves it and pays it.
  • Never picks the as-of date, the cash available or the company for you. A bank balance is not available cash. It asks every run.
  • Never proposes a real payment run from test data. If any figure is test data, the run is an illustration only, and says so at the top.
  • Never saves vendor bank details. You confirm a change through a channel the vendor did not supply. Only then can a payment go to the new account.
Words you'll hear

Seven terms, in plain English

Accounts payable
The bills you owe to suppliers and have not paid yet. Accountants write A/P or AP.
Aging
A table that sorts every unpaid bill by how late it is.
Aging bucket
One band of lateness, for example 1–30 days late. The skill's default bands go from "not yet due" to "over 120 days".
Payment run
The group of payments you send at one time, often once a week.
Cash available
The amount you choose to spend in this run. It is your decision, not the bank balance.
2/10 Net 30
Payment terms: 2% off if you pay within 10 days, otherwise the full amount is due in 30 days1.
Credit hold
You keep a bill unpaid on purpose, for example during a dispute. The run never pays it.
