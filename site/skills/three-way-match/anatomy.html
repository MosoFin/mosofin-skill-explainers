How the three-way match is built, and what it does

Was one 729-line file; now a 139-line card and six reference files. It matches purchase orders, goods receipts and vendor invoices before a bill is paid, and since the 27 Oct 2026 bookkeeping audit a two-way match also checks billed quantity against the order, and the Pay, Hold or Reject call comes with a matched and a disputed amount. Its distinctive move is that it names no evidence tools at all: Gate 2 asks three capability questions of the live catalogue — are purchase orders exposed, are item receipts, is approval status — and the answers decide which of fifteen exception tests can run. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, and what both audits found.

Bookkeeping audit, 27 Oct 2026

Nine findings from a senior-bookkeeper review: eight fixed, one partly, in d9bdc22

An independent audit read the card and every reference file as a senior bookkeeper would, and judged the skill fit with fixes. What mattered most: on a two-way match no test compared billed quantity with ordered quantity, so an invoice billing more than was ordered could come back Pay.

High · fixed

A two-way match had no quantity test at all

The only quantity tests were 3 “Quantity overbilling — Invoice qty > GRN qty” and 4 “Short-shipment billed in full — Invoice qty > GRN qty”, both needing the receipt leg. Nothing compared invoice quantity with the PO, so on a two-way match, the common case, 120 units billed against an order for 100 could come back “Pay”, and the coverage sheet would not even list the test as not run.

Fix. Test 3 now compares invoice quantity, cumulative on the PO line, with PO quantity and runs on the PO leg alone; the note says this is the quantity test a two-way match relies on, and for services billed hours or units are compared with the PO and the service confirmation. The card says billed quantity is still checked against the order. The count stays at fifteen.

Standard accounts-payable practice: a two-way match compares PO price and PO quantity with the invoice. references/method.md · Step 4, test 3 · SKILL.md
High · fixed

The GRNI figure could be overstated or double-counted

Exception 7 matched receipt lines only against the invoices this run read, then reported the total as the period-end accrual. A receipt whose bill was already paid or billed in another period showed as received-not-billed; a QuickBooks Desktop item receipt already posts to Accounts Payable, so accruing it again double-counted the liability; and no valuation basis was set.

Fix. Receipts are matched against all bills for the PO, open or paid, in any period; receipts already posted to Accounts Payable are excluded; each line is valued at received quantity × PO unit price in PO currency. A draft reversing accrual is proposed: DR Inventory or the PO line's expense account, CR Accrued liabilities – GRNI, reversing on day 1 of the next period.

Accrual basis and standard cut-off practice (ASC 405 named by the audit): accrue only receipts with no recorded liability. references/method.md · exception 7, Sheet 5 · edge-cases.md
Medium · fixed

The card promised matched and disputed amounts, but the method never worked them out

“recommends Pay, Hold or Reject with the matched and disputed amounts”, yet Step 5 gave no rule for choosing and nothing defined either amount, and nothing stopped a Pay on goods whose receipt was never confirmed.

Fix. Step 5 has a decision rule: Reject for a vendor mismatch or a duplicate; Hold for an open Critical or High exception, unconfirmed approval, or goods with no receipt leg; Pay otherwise. Matched amount = Σ min(PO, GRN, invoice qty) × PO price over lines within tolerance; disputed = invoice total − matched. Sheet 1 shows both and Sheet 3 gains an Amount column.

Standard accounts-payable control practice: no payment of goods without proof of receipt. references/method.md · Step 5, Sheets 1 and 3
Medium · fixed

Exceptions 3 and 4 were the same test, so one line was flagged twice

Both rows read “Invoice qty > GRN qty”, so a short shipment billed in full raised two High exceptions and inflated the count.

Fix. Test 4 is now GRN qty < PO qty and invoice qty = PO qty; test 3 excludes lines test 4 covers, and a rule says one line raises one quantity exception.

Standard practice: one condition, one exception. references/method.md · Step 4
Medium · fixed

On the upload path the cumulative and duplicate tests had nothing to compare against

Progress billing, partial billing and exception 9 need invoices already billed or paid against the PO, but the upload checklist asked only for the current invoices, so one progress invoice gave a clean cumulative test even if earlier bills had used up the PO.

Fix. A new upload row, “Prior bills and payments against these POs”, required for progress and partial billing and the duplicate test; the method's Inputs table lists it, and output.md Section D lists the cumulative test and exception 9 as not run when it is missing.

The skill's own rule that both paths run the same checks; standard practice on progress billing. SKILL.md upload checklist · references/method.md · output.md
Medium · fixed

The vendor master was requested for payment-terms checks, but no test used it

Terms were extracted from PO and invoice and the vendor master was read, but none of the fifteen tests compared them, so the user uploaded a file that changed nothing. Shorter invoice terms (Net 15 against Net 45) send cash out early.

Fix. Step 2 now compares invoice terms with the PO and vendor master, reported as a Low note alongside the fifteen tests.

Routine accounts-payable review practice. references/method.md · Step 2
Low · 2 fixed, 1 partly

Three smaller corrections

  • Approval matrix “Recommended” on the card, “Optional” in the method, and Gate 3 asked again for what Stage 1 had covered. Now “Recommended” in both, and Gate 3 asks “only those not already answered at Stage 1”.
