This credit
For a sales return

The page calculates the expected tax as the credit net amount times the rate of the original, rounded to the cent. When a credit is more than the open balance, the rest goes on account. The page always credits Accounts Receivable, because a credit on account is a credit balance in receivables. For a goodwill credit, the page assumes that the credit has an original invoice. Without an original, the page cannot mirror the tax. NRV is net realizable value. You supply it, because the books cannot show that goods came back broken. To test the limit check, enter a net amount of 3,000, a rate of 7.5% and earlier credits of 1,075. Then enter an open balance of 2,150 and a credit net amount of 2,200. Credits to date of 3,440 are 215 more than the original 3,225.

Using it

What it asks, needs and gives back

Asks firstUse Mosofin for this run, or upload the data yourself? What type of credit is it, and for a chargeback, which kind? Which original invoice or bill? How much is the credit? What is the reason? Which tax jurisdiction? Which period? Who approves a credit that is more than the original?
NeedsThe skill needs the credit document and the original invoice or bill. It needs the type, the reason and the amount from you. It needs the chart of accounts, and the jurisdiction when the original had tax. The aging detail lets it list unapplied credits.
Gives backThe skill gives back six sheets: the credit register, draft entries, application tracking, unapplied credits, validation exceptions and coverage. It asks before it makes an Excel file.
NeverThe skill never reads your data before you answer its questions and say yes to its read-back. It never writes to any system, and it never guesses the reason, the type or the tax. It never proposes a second entry on application, and never proposes a refund on mock data.
