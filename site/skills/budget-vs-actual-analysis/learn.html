How it thinks

Fifteen rules the whole skill runs on

Every level below applies one or more of these rules. The examples use one made-up company, Northbrook Trading, a US entity that keeps its books in US dollars. Its fiscal year is the calendar year. The page compares its September actuals with the "FY26 Board Budget". Its thresholds are $5,000 and 10%. These figures are the company's own choices. No standard sets them.

Part 1: the variance on each line

LineTypeBudgetActual

The zero-budget rule for "both": a line with a budget of $0 has no percent. Under "both", this page counts that line as passing when its dollar variance passes the dollar threshold. This rule is the page's own choice. The skill does not state one. If you leave both thresholds empty, the page marks every variance above zero, as the skill does when you decline to set thresholds.

The page computes variance = actual − budget, and percent = variance ÷ |budget|. A revenue line is F when the actual is higher. An expense line is U when the actual is higher. The "Northbrook, September" preset gives a net effect on profit of −$23,900 U. It needs commentary on 6 of 7 lines under "either" and on 4 of 7 lines under "both".

Part 2: volume and price

Budget
Actual

Volume = (actual units − budget units) × budget price. Price = (actual price − budget price) × actual units. The two parts add to the total variance when mix and exchange rates do not apply. The "Northbrook revenue" preset gives volume +$60,000 F and price −$28,000 U, a total of +$32,000. A split from this calculator shows the arithmetic only. A person still gives the cause.

Using it

What it asks, needs and gives back

Asks firstDo you want to use Mosofin for this run, or upload the data yourself? Which budget, by name? Which period, and at what grain? What are your materiality thresholds, as an amount and a percentage? Which one-time items fall in the period, and who owns each line? Which driver data do the books not hold?
NeedsThe skill needs the actuals and the named budget, by line and by month. The prior-year actuals, the chart of accounts and the transactions behind large lines make the result better. You type in the thresholds, the one-offs and the owners, because no ledger holds them.
Gives backThe skill gives back six sheets: the variance summary, the commentary, the driver detail, the full-year outlook, the waterfall data and a coverage sheet. It asks before it makes an Excel file.
NeverThe skill never revises a budget, posts a reclassification or updates a forecast. It never chooses the period, the budget version or a threshold for you. It never invents a cause. It never saves the figures, the commentary or the outlook.
