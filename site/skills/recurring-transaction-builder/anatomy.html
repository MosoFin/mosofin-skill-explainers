How the recurring transaction builder is built, and what it does

A 164-line card with six reference files behind it, split from a 771-line SKILL.md: 159 lines at the split, five more from the audit fixes. It builds the schedule for a journal entry, bill or invoice that repeats on a cadence and computes every future occurrence, with rolls, escalations and memos resolved. With the books it validates the template against the live chart, checks whether the recurrence already exists, and audits whether existing recurring entries actually ran. It never creates, posts or schedules anything. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, and the eight findings from the 27 Oct bookkeeping audit.

Audit → findings

Eight findings, all fixed in 46708f0

An independent senior-bookkeeper audit on 27 Oct 2026 judged the skill fit with fixes: the schedule arithmetic, the “a schedule is not a template” honesty and the Pending double-load control are strong, but the upload path dropped every check that protects money. Every finding was accepted and fixed in one commit.

High · fixed

H1 — The upload path declared the protective checks impossible instead of asking for the files

The card said “Without a connection the validation, duplicate check, discovery and run audit cannot run; the schedule is computed only,” and the upload checklist asked only for typed parameters. Every file those checks need is a standard QuickBooks or Xero export, so an upload user got an unchecked 24-bill rent file.

Fix. The upload checklist now asks for the chart of accounts and item list, the vendor or customer list, the tax code list, the recurring-template list and 12–24 months of counterparty transactions. Steps 2b–2d and 5b run on those files, labelled user-supplied; only a check whose file was not supplied is NOT VALIDATED, and Sheet 1 says which.

Basis: the skill's own protocol that both paths run identical checks · SKILL.md upload checklist · references/method.md verdicts and Step 6
Medium · fixed

M1 — The duplicate check ignored the platform's own recurring-template list

Whether a recurrence “already exists” was answered only from posted history. A template set up last week, set to start next month, or saved as a reminder or draft has no postings and passed the check.

Fix. Step 2c reads the platform's recurring-template list first where the Gate 2 catalogue exposes a read, otherwise asks for the export, and matches counterparty, amount and next date before reading history. Step 5b seeds its register from that list on a first run.

Basis: how QuickBooks Online and Xero hold scheduled, reminder and draft recurring templates · references/method.md Steps 2c and 5b
Medium · fixed

M2 — Advance-billed subscriptions were coded to revenue, and the deferral misstated

Recurring invoices went to a revenue account, and the text said an annual-in-advance invoice “produces eleven months of deferred revenue on day one.” On the invoice date all twelve months are unearned.

Fix. An invoice covering a service period beyond the invoice month is coded to Deferred Revenue with a release schedule by service period; the sentence now reads “twelve months of deferred revenue on the invoice date, released one month at a time.”

Basis: ASC 606-10-45-2 and IFRS 15.106 (contract liability, recognised as the service is provided) · references/method.md Step 5
Medium · fixed

M3 — A business-day roll could push a period-end entry into the next period

One roll direction applied to a last-day-of-month accrual rolled 31 August 2025, a Sunday, forward to 1 September, so the entry landed in the wrong month and the reversal timing broke.

Fix. A roll never moves an occurrence out of its period. Period-end JEs keep the calendar month-end date; where a forward roll would cross a month end, that occurrence rolls back, noted in its memo.

Basis: period-end cut-off and the modified-following business-day convention · references/method.md Step 3
Medium · fixed

M4 — Intake missed what a bill or invoice template needs

Bills and invoices need payment terms, and a QuickBooks Online invoice line takes a product or service item, not a GL account. Neither was asked or validated, so Step 5 could not produce the due date it promised.

Fix. Question F now asks for accounts, item or product/service item by type, plus tax code; a new F2 asks for payment terms. The Inputs table matches, Step 2b checks the item exists and is active with its account, and Step 5 sets the due date from the terms.

Basis: how QuickBooks Online codes invoice lines by product or service item · SKILL.md F, F2 · references/method.md Inputs, Steps 2b and 5
Medium · fixed

M5 — Escalating rent built straight to Rent Expense with no lease-accounting flag

The read-back example coded rent with a 3% annual increase to Rent Expense. For an entity reporting under US GAAP or IFRS, an escalating lease is not expensed as billed.

Fix. A new edge case, “Lease payments with escalations”: the schedule is the cash or bill side; under GAAP or IFRS, or for a lease over 12 months, flag “lease accounting may apply — expense is not the billed amount”, ask the reporting basis and code per the user's accountant.

Basis: ASC 842-20-25-6 (straight-line operating-lease cost) and IFRS 16 · references/edge-cases.md · SKILL.md Stage 4
Low · all fixed

L1, L2 — two smaller corrections, both fixed

  • L1 — Backfilled schedules could land in closed periods. Backfilling was allowed with no check against the lock date. Step 2 now asks the closed-through date and marks any occurrence on or before it “Closed period — do not load without accountant approval”. Basis: standard close practice
  • L2 — Pro-ration and the holiday calendar were missing from the Inputs table. The card collects “the Inputs table in references/method.md”, which had no rows for either. Both rows are now in the table. Basis: internal consistency between card and method
