How the journal entry review is built, and what it does

A 162-line card with seven reference files behind it, split from a 941-line SKILL.md: 160 lines at the split, two more from the audit fixes. It screens every journal entry in a period for the signs that tend to accompany error or manipulation, scores them with weighted combinations, and puts the few worth a closer look at the top. It identifies risk indicators and never concludes that fraud occurred. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, and the seven findings from the 27 Oct bookkeeping audit.

Audit → findings

Seven findings, all fixed in 510b0ca

An independent senior-bookkeeper audit on 27 Oct 2026 judged the skill fit with fixes: the criteria, the scoring and the “a flag is a question” stance are professional, but the skill called its screen complete without ever proving the population was complete. Every finding was accepted and fixed in one commit.

High · fixed

H1 — “The screen was complete” with no test that the population is complete

Complete meant only that every row the read returned was screened; nothing tied those rows to the ledger. On QuickBooks Online and Xero a journal-entry search also returns only journal-type transactions, so hand-keyed deposits, cheques, transfers and inventory adjustments fell outside the screen, and the manual-versus-system split could not be computed.

Fix. Step 1 now proves the population first: the extract is summed by account and agreed to the period's ledger movement, and the difference reported. A journal-type read is flagged as excluding other manual transaction types. The header says “screen complete” only on a nil tie-out; the upload path asks for the all-types Journal report and the opening and closing trial balance.

Basis: AU-C 240.32 and A47–A48; PCAOB AS 2401.58–61 (journal-entry testing starts from a complete population) · references/method.md Steps 1 and 8 · SKILL.md · output.md · overview.md · edge-cases.md
Medium · fixed

M1 — The back-dating test asked for fields QuickBooks and Xero do not have

The test compared “posting date” with “effective date”. In QuickBooks Online and Xero the meaningful pair is the transaction date against the created timestamp, and the poster is “Created by”, so the test could compare two columns that both mean transaction date and pass silently.

Fix. Back-dating is now a created date and time later than the transaction date, [gated] on a created timestamp and NOT RUN without one. The upload row names the exports: QuickBooks Journal report or Audit Log with Create date and Created by; Xero Journal Report with Created/Posted.

Basis: how QuickBooks Online and Xero record the transaction date and the created timestamp · references/method.md Step 2 · edge-cases.md · SKILL.md
Medium · fixed

M2 — The close date and post-close entries were never collected

Three timing indicators need the date the books were closed through and the date of the close, and reversal pairs need the month after the period. Stage 1 asked for neither, yet “entries to prior closed periods” was marked [auto].

Fix. Stage 1 has a new question I for the closed-through date and the close date. Step 0 and the upload list read the first month after the period. Without a close date the after-close and prior-closed tests are NOT RUN; prior-closed is now [gated].

Basis: AU-C 240.A47 (entries made at the end of a period or after closing are the core cut-off population) · SKILL.md Stage 1 · references/method.md Steps 0 and 2
Medium · fixed

M3 — No approval data and no approval control were treated as the same

The skill only asked for an approval log and otherwise reported four indicators NOT RUN. Most small businesses on QuickBooks or Xero have no approval step at all, and “not run” reads as “the control may be fine”.

Fix. Question G now asks whether anyone must approve a manual journal entry, then whether it can be exported. No approval step is recorded as “Control not in place” on the controls sheet; a step with no log stays NOT RUN.

Basis: AU-C 265 (a missing control is a design deficiency) · SKILL.md G · references/method.md Steps 2 and 7
Medium · fixed

M4 — The follow-up sheet had no owner, due date or closure evidence

Step 6 recorded the entry, the flag, the explanation and a resolution, but nobody was named to answer and no supporting document was referenced at closure.

Fix. Sheet 6 columns are now JE number, why flagged, asked of (role), date asked, due, support obtained (document reference), resolution, and reviewed by and date.

Basis: standard audit workpaper practice for clearing flagged items · references/method.md Step 8, Sheet 6
Low · all fixed

L1, L2 — two smaller corrections, both fixed

  • L1 — Indicator counts did not add up. Nineteen bullets were described as including four approval-based ones (there were two), and “fifteen run as queries” counted four [gated] items. The approval bullets are now split into four, for twenty-one in all: ten run as queries, seven need a threshold or a field, four need approval data. Basis: internal consistency between the criteria sheet and the criteria-not-run sheet
  • L2 — The topside question was pitched at a group. “Do topside or consolidation entries exist?” is jargon to an owner. Question E now asks about adjustments made outside the books by the accountant, in tax software or in a consolidation spreadsheet, keeping “topside” as the term. Basis: plain language for a single-entity business
