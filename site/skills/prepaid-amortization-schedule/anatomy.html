How the prepaid amortization schedule is built, and what it does

A 155-line card with six reference files behind it, split on 25 Oct 2026 from a 925-line file. It builds and maintains the schedule for expenses paid in advance, spreads each over its coverage period, drafts the monthly entry and ties the running balance to the general ledger. Its premise is that the ledger holds the prepaid balance but not the schedule, so a roll-forward is never reported as a reconciliation. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, what the 27 Oct bookkeeping audit found and how each finding was fixed.

Nine findings, all fixed on 27 Oct, with the standard behind each

An independent senior-bookkeeper audit read the card and every reference file and called it the strongest of the four skills it reviewed in that batch; each finding was checked against the skill before acting. Severity is the auditor's.

High · fixed

The correcting entries were wrong or missing for the very errors the skill finds

Step 6 said “If the entire amount was already expensed in error, reverse the expense to set up the prepaid asset.” A $12,000 annual premium expensed in March and found in September has six months left, so a full reversal sets up $6,000 too much; a charge in a closed prior year would distort this year's P&L; and missed months and expired residue got no entry at all.

Fix. The correction sets up only the unexpired portion at the correction date, with a worked example. If the charge fell in a closed prior year, the user is asked whether it is material: if not, it goes through current-year expense; if so, it is referred as a prior-period adjustment. Step 7 and Sheet 4 add catch-up entries for missed months and expired residue.

Behind it: ASC 250 and IAS 8: a material amount belonging to a closed prior year is a prior-period error, not a current-year credit.

Medium · fixed

The tax-inside-the-asset test would fire on insurance and on unregistered vendors

The test reported an error whenever prepaid additions equalled the gross bill in a jurisdiction that allows recovery. Insurance, the commonest prepaid, is exempt from VAT/GST in many regimes, insurance premium tax is not recoverable, and unregistered vendors charge no recoverable tax, so gross is correct there.

Fix. The test fires only where the bill carries a recoverable tax code or amount; exempt, out-of-scope and non-recoverable tax belongs in the asset.

Behind it: VAT and GST rules in the UK, EU and Australia, which exempt insurance; insurance premium tax is not recoverable.

Medium · fixed

The upload checklist did not ask for what three of the promised tests need

It asked for the “Expense ledger for the period”: one month cannot give a monthly average, so the expensed-in-one-hit scan could not run. Bills with tax lines and vendor credits and receipts were not requested, so the tax test and refund detection did nothing on uploads.

Fix. The expense row asks for 12–24 months of GL detail and says the scan is not performed without it; a new row asks for the bills behind each addition with tax lines and for vendor credits and receipts. Tests not run are named on the coverage sheet.

Behind it: the skill's own protocol: both paths run the same checks, and a check not run is named.

Medium · fixed

The items could fail to explain the opening balance

A fixed 24-month read misses a three-year policy paid 30 months ago that still carries a balance, and the client's existing schedule was asked for only on the upload path.

Fix. Step 0 reads back to the oldest item still carried (at least 24 months), uses the existing schedule, and confirms the items sum to the opening GL balance, any remainder a named exception. Intake D asks for the existing schedule on both paths.

Behind it: standard schedule practice: explain the full opening balance item by item before adding new items.

Medium · fixed

The installment option grossed up an executory contract

The edge case allowed setting up “the entire contract value … as prepaid with an offsetting payable for the unpaid installments”, which books a liability for services not yet received.

Fix. The grossed-up option is deleted. Each installment paid or invoiced is its own prepaid over the coverage it buys; unbilled future installments are a commitment, disclosed if material; leases follow the lease standards.

Behind it: US GAAP and IFRS treatment of executory contracts (not recognised until one side performs); ASC 842 and IFRS 16 for leases.
