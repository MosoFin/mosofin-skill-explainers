The structure of the prepaid amortization schedule skill, and what it does

The skill was one 925-line file. On main it is now a 155-line card and six reference files. Pull request #1 merged them on 26 October 2026. A bookkeeping-audit commit, 4591599, then changed the method on 27 October 2026. The skill reads the prepaid account, but the ledger does not hold the coverage dates. So the skill always asks the owner for them, and it audits the account from the ledger alone. It posts nothing. This page shows how the files connect, the run stage by stage, and the source behind each step. It also shows the audit for the skill owner: one check passed and eleven open findings.

The audit checked the seven sources on 28 Oct 2026 through search results. Nobody opened the pages. Sources 1, 5, 6 and 7 are US, sources 2 and 3 are international, and source 4 is UK. Sources 5 and 6 are about tax, and book amortization can differ from tax timing. The pages give no tax figure for the example company. Some items have no outside authority behind them. They are the four ledger tests, the lumpiness ratio, the three-way tie-out and its variance shapes, and the verdict tags. The seven sheets and the label PROPOSED — NOT POSTED have none either. The worked example uses made-up figures. Ask your accountant what applies to you. This is not tax advice.

A6. Step 7 ties expired residue to a test that may not fire

Step 7 drafts catch-up entries for expired residue "from Test 0d, once the items are identified". Test 0d fires only when the balance is above twelve months of additions. In the worked example, 550 of expired residue is in the account and Test 0d does not fire. The schedule finds it. The worked example showed this gap.

A8. Does a schedule that the skill builds count as leg 3?

The upload row says that without the existing schedule "the tie-out is a roll-forward only". But Stage 4 builds a schedule from the coverage dates, and the rules say that only the schedule tests the balance. The files do not say if a schedule built from the owner's dates counts as leg 3. The worked example assumes that it does.
