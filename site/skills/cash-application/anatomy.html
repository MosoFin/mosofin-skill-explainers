How cash application is built, and what it does

A 155-line card with seven reference files behind it, split on 25 Oct 2026 from a 909-line file. It decides which open invoices each customer payment was meant to settle, works out why a short pay is short, parks overpayments as customer credit and drafts the entry, all as proposals. A senior-bookkeeper audit on 27 Oct found a short pay on a home-currency invoice being booked as an FX loss instead of collected; that and seven other findings are fixed. This page shows where the weight sits, the run step by step, and what the audit found.

Bookkeeping audit, 27 Oct 2026

Eight findings from a senior-bookkeeper review, all eight fixed in 38aef1c

An independent audit read the card and every reference file as a senior bookkeeper would, and judged the skill fit with fixes. Each finding was checked against the skill before acting; every one below is fixed, none partly fixed or rejected.

High · fixed

A “rounding / FX difference” short pay went to FX gain or loss whatever the invoice currency

The short-pay table read “Rounding / FX difference — Apply payment + book FX Gain/Loss for the difference”. On a USD invoice paid short because the customer's bank converted from EUR at a poor rate, the entity has no exchange exposure: the customer simply paid too little. The row turned an uncollected receivable into an FX loss and bypassed the skill's own rule that an unexplained residual stays open.

Fix. Split in two: an FX difference on a foreign-currency invoice goes to Step 5 (invoice rate against receipt rate) and FX gain or loss; a conversion shortfall or rounding on a base-currency invoice stays open, or, within the user's tolerance, becomes a proposed small-balance write-off to the account the user's policy names, for approval.

ASC 830 and IAS 21: exchange differences arise only on monetary items denominated in a foreign currency. references/method.md · Step 3 short-pay table
Medium · fixed

The tolerance was collected but no step used it

Intake D asks for a short- and over-pay tolerance because it “decides what counts as a short or over pay at all”, yet Steps 2–4 never tested a residual against it, so every 50-cent short pay became a collections exception.

Fix. Step 3 tests each residual first: within tolerance, a small-balance adjustment is proposed and marked “within tolerance”, with the account asked from the user's policy; above it, the reason table applies; with no tolerance supplied, every residual is above it. The card's “never absorbs” rule names the exception.

Standard cash-application policy: write off residuals within tolerance, route the rest to collections. references/method.md · Step 3 · SKILL.md rules
Medium · fixed

Payments in an uploaded file were never checked against payments already recorded

In QuickBooks Online, bank-feed rules and payment sync apps often record some of a day's receipts automatically, so a lockbox or deposit file overlaps the books. Applying a payment that is already applied produces a duplicate receipt, double-crediting receivables and doubling the cash.

Fix. Step 1 compares each file row with recorded payments and deposits (customer or payer, amount, date within three days, reference); rows already recorded move to the review population with no new entry, and the count removed is reported. Intake B says so.

Standard bookkeeping practice: de-duplicate a batch against the ledger before posting. references/method.md · Step 1 · SKILL.md intake B
Medium · fixed

The “review of already-applied payments” path had no procedure

The card offered a review as one of its two jobs, but Steps 2–6 only described applying new payments, and matching against open balances fails when the invoices are already closed.

Fix. A new Review mode section in method.md: customers carrying both unapplied payments or credits and open invoices, with the application proposed; levels 1–3 re-run for each recorded payment against the invoices it was applied to; discounts checked against terms; unapplied cash and unidentified deposits aged. The card's intake B and edge-cases.md point to it.

Standard accounts-receivable review practice. references/method.md · Review mode · edge-cases.md · SKILL.md
Medium · fixed

The output did not prove its own totals

Sheet 1 listed totals processed, applied, parked, unidentified and short-pay residual, but nothing required them to add up or tied the population to the file control total or the deposit, so a dropped or double-counted row went unseen.

Fix. Sheet 1 has a control tie-out: total payments (the file control total or deposit total) = applied to receivables − discounts − fees − withholding tax ± FX + customer credit + unidentified, with the difference shown even when nil.

Standard practice: a batch control total on every cash-application run. references/method.md · Step 7, Sheet 1
Low · 3 fixed

Three smaller corrections

  • The entry template was ambiguous and had no unidentified-deposit entry. Debits now come first; the receivables line reads “invoice amounts settled (= cash + discount + fee + WHT ± FX)”; “Unidentified: DR Bank (or Undeposited Funds), CR Unapplied cash” is added, with a note that batched receipts debit Undeposited Funds so the deposit matches the statement.
  • The tolerance default contradicted itself (“Default exact” beside “never default the tolerance”). Now “never invent a tolerance; with none supplied, match exact and say so” in gates.md and on the card.
  • FIFO was a default in Step 2 but “confirm rather than assume” at intake. Now oldest first only if the user confirmed FIFO; otherwise the candidate allocation is presented for approval.
